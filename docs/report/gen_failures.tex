\chapter{Every failure}\label{ch:failures}

This chapter is the project's defect register: every fault found between
2026-07-14 and 2026-08-26, in the order it was found, with its mechanism and
its resolution. It is assembled from the 7\,099-line diagnostic history, the
commit log and the test suite.

Three kinds of entry appear, and they are deliberately not separated, because
in this project they were rarely distinguishable at the moment of discovery:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{System defects} --- the robot, the safety layer or the task did
      the wrong thing.
\item \textbf{Instrument defects} --- the measurement was wrong, and the
      system was not. \emph{These outnumber the first kind.}
\item \textbf{Findings} --- nothing is broken, and the platform cannot do the
      thing that was asked.
\end{itemize}

Entries are numbered \textbf{D1} onward and referred to by number elsewhere in
this report.

\dperiod{2026-07-31 --- the first build}{Teleoperation, collision safety, the
gripper, the e-stop, and the first attempt at a real-robot bring-up.}

\dfct{Commanded pose is not a proxy for the end effector under motion}
{The only recording with a moving master (right arm, 2\,097 samples) measured
the software mapping at gain $+1.034$ and the \emph{actual} end-effector gain
at $+0.100$: tracking RMS 0.0396\,m, max 0.2956\,m. Every earlier
sub-millimetre tracking figure had been taken with the master at rest and
proved nothing about tracking. \textit{Mechanism:} that recording ran with
\code{anchored} orientation, where IK was failing over 90\,\% of the time, so
the follower had almost nothing to publish. \textit{Consequence:} no
experiment may use commanded pose as a proxy for end-effector pose; all
position measures come from tf2.}

\dfct{The wearer was invisible to MoveIt}
{\code{avoid\_collisions=True} was set on every IK request, and
\file{human\_backpack.xacro} carried 27 \code{<visual>} elements and exactly
\emph{one} \code{<collision>}; no human link appeared in the SRDF at all.
MoveIt cannot avoid what it cannot see, so the flag had been doing nothing for
the person wearing the machine. \textit{Fix:} 13 collision elements added,
paired with 36 SRDF exclusions for the permanently-adjacent mount links, with
everything distal of \code{half\_arm\_1} left enabled.}

\dfct{The right gripper exported zero command interfaces}
{\code{right\_robotiq\_85\_left\_knuckle\_joint/position} read
\code{[unavailable] [unclaimed]}. The URDF was correct. \textit{Mechanism:} the
vendor macro declared \code{<gpio name="reactivate\_gripper">}
\emph{unprefixed}, so on a dual-arm robot both hardware components registered
the same GPIO interface names and the second to load exported nothing. An
earlier vendor patch had prefixed the hardware \emph{name} and missed the GPIO.}

\dfct{Three independent clutch glitches}
{Button bounce produced double toggles; a \emph{moving} reference could be
latched; and pre-disengage filter state bled into the first frames after
re-engagement. \textit{Fix:} 50\,ms debounce, a quasi-static gate (2\,mm over
100\,ms), a reference averaged over five frames, and an explicit EMA reset on
engage.}

\dfct{Both Robotiq drivers defaulted to the same serial port}
{Both real gripper drivers defaulted to \code{COM\_port=/dev/ttyUSB0} and would
have fought over one device the first time real hardware was attached. Found by
a full scan of \code{<gpio>}, \code{<joint>} and \code{<sensor>} names across
all four \code{ros2\_control} components.}

\dfct{The task volume in the brief is not reachable}
{The volume named in the project brief measures 5.6\,\% IK success with
collisions on and 11.1\,\% with them off. The same $0.5\times0.3\times0.2$\,m
box moved to the arm's own side at chest height measures 77.8--100\,\%.
\textit{Finding, not defect:} a back-mounted arm at $z=1.25$ cannot work a
table at $z=0.75$--$0.95$ across the body centreline.}

\dfct{The right arm's home sat 5\dg{} from the $\pm\pi$ seam}
{Seam margin 0.087\,rad on \code{joint\_5}, where a wrap executes as a
358\dg{} rotation, with 29.1\dg{} of headroom against a 30\dg{} target.
\textit{Fix:} home replaced by the left pose mirrored about $x=0$ --- mirroring
the \emph{pose} and solving IK, not negating joints, because both arms use the
same non-mirrored vendor macro. Seam margin $\rightarrow$ 1.268\,rad.}

\dfct{No table height reaches 80\,\% IK, and the binding constraint is not reach}
{Best 77.8\,\% at chest height; 16.7\,\% at table height. Of 18 table-height
samples: JOINT/IK 9, orientation 1, collision 1, reach \emph{1}. The Gen3
spans the distance easily (0.74\,m required against 0.902\,m of reach) and
cannot \emph{fold} into the required configuration inside its joint limits.
No mount orientation fixes that, because orientation does not change how far
the arm has to fold.}

\dfct{Tilting the mount down makes it worse}
{Swept without touching the URDF (rotating the base by $\delta$ is exactly
equivalent to rotating targets by $-\delta$). Aiming the base downward points
the arm into the wearer's body, so it must reach further round. The one angle
that improves both table and chest reach ($+0.3$\,rad) was recorded and
deliberately not applied, because applying it invalidates \code{P\_HOME}, the
anchor and every clearance figure.}

\dfct{Pointwise IK scoring chose the worst home}
{The right home had been selected because it scored 100\,\% \emph{pointwise}.
Under continuous reachability --- each step seeded from the previous solution,
as the follower does --- it is the worse of the two arms: 15 of 26 directions
usable, isotropy 0.32. \textit{Mechanism:} pointwise IK asks ``could the arm
get there from anywhere''; teleoperation asks ``can it get there from where it
already is, without a branch switch''.}

\dfct{MoveIt's IK plugin ignores the constraints field}
{An \code{OrientationConstraint} of $(0.2, 0.2, \pi)$ returned \emph{exactly}
the same IK success as an exact pose --- left 79.3\,\% both ways, right
68.9\,\% both ways, over 270 calls. Identical to the digit is not a
coincidence: this \code{/compute\_ik} setup is not constraint-aware. The tilt
orientation mode was therefore implemented and not shipped.}

\dfct{\code{real\_robot} mode had never successfully run}
{\code{ik\_follower\_node} read \code{self.max\_vel} nine lines before
assigning it, raising \code{AttributeError} on every \code{real\_robot:=true}
start. \textit{And the fix exposed a second defect:} once the crash was
repaired, the later line would have \emph{overwritten} the 0.10\,rad/s safety
cap the block had just applied. A clamp discarded one line later is worse than
no clamp, because the warning still prints.}

\dfct{New launch files were silently not installed}
{\file{setup.py} listed launch files by hand, so \file{mock\_real.launch.py}
and \file{real\_arms.launch.py} did not install and did not error. Replaced
with a glob.}

\dfct{Kortex driver deactivation is one-way}
{Deactivating the hardware component tears down the API router;
\code{on\_activate} then fails permanently with \code{KBasicException: Router
is not active}. \textit{Consequence:} the driver-level e-stop cannot be undone
in-process --- recovery is a relaunch, and the gate is built around that.}

\dfct{\code{/real/joint\_states} is frozen while the component is inactive}
{313 samples over 5\,s gave one distinct value per joint, peak-to-peak
$0.000\mathrm{e}{+}00$, identical to a reading taken 40 minutes earlier. The
broadcaster keeps publishing at 100\,Hz and the content is a cache. Any
hold-versus-limp test done through that topic reports ``holding'' regardless of
what the arm does. Same zero-variance signature that identified the dead
\code{j7} potentiometer.}

\dperiod{2026-08-05 --- the mount, and the hardening pass}{Two mount fixes, the
proximal collision, fault injection from 8/10 to 14/14, and the two safety
defects that had hidden for months.}

\dfct{The mount angle was in the wrong \code{rpy} slot}
{At the legacy home the real arms point forward; in simulation the same angles
put the left hand 0.49\,m behind the wearer and the right hand 0.24\,m above a
1.75\,m person's head. \textit{Mechanism:} the mount was
\code{rpy="0 $\pm$0.6 0"}, and in this file's convention $x$ is lateral and $y$
is fore/aft --- so a rotation about $y$ is a \emph{lateral splay}, not a
forward pitch. The old mount splayed both arms 34.4\dg{} outward with zero
forward tilt. \textit{The tell:} the hand-written sign flip on the right arm.
A pure forward tilt is a roll, and a roll needs no flip under mirroring.}

\dfct{The arm's shape was ruled out before the mount was blamed}
{Every link's pose relative to its own \code{base\_link} was compared against
the stock vendor description at the same angles: maximum deviation
$0.000000000$\,m and $0.0000024$\dg. Three further checks ruled out a
joint-zero convention error, including reading the vendor driver's own source
(degrees to radians plus a wrap, nothing else). \textit{Recorded because the
alternative hypothesis --- per-joint offsets --- would have been a large and
wrong change.}}

\dfct{Link naming is viewer-perspective, not anatomical}
{\code{left\_leg}, \code{left\_mount} and \code{human\_left\_upper\_arm} all sit
at \emph{positive} $x$, i.e. on the wearer's anatomical \emph{right}.
Kinematically irrelevant; decisive when matching a simulated arm to a physical
arm in the room.}

\dfct{The mirror-symmetry criterion is unsatisfiable, and it is a proof}
{With mirror-symmetric mounts, $|\text{right}\,P_{HOME} -
\text{mirror}(\text{left}\,P_{HOME})|$ \emph{does not depend on the mount
rotation at all} --- the rotation cancels algebraically. It is 1.3837\,m for
every mirror-symmetric mount, old or new. \textit{Cause:} the home joint angles
park the two arms asymmetrically. Fixing it needs a re-parked right arm in
hardware, not a different mount.}

\dfct{The proximal collision: 48 of 48 IK poses refused, including the pose the arm was standing in}
{The first candidate mount was applied and \code{/compute\_ik} returned
\code{NO\_IK\_SOLUTION} for every pose on both arms.
\code{/check\_state\_validity} named it at once:
\code{left\_shoulder\_link} against \code{human\_left\_upper\_arm}, depth
0.0240. \textit{Mechanism:} the offline search scored clearance from
\emph{distal link origins}, which cannot see a proximal link and cannot see a
contact nowhere near a link origin. \textit{This is a mechanical finding:} the
mount origin is 0.1342\,m from the wearer's upper-arm axis --- 38\,mm of
surface clearance --- against a base tube 46\,mm in radius and 171\,mm long,
moved by no joint.}

\dfct{The first mount fix routed the arm through the wearer}
{It searched only \emph{endpoint} criteria and had no path or intermediate-link
constraint, so it found rotations satisfying the endpoint by putting
\code{base\_link} and \code{shoulder\_link} \textbf{75\,mm inside the torso},
with ten link pairs in collision. \textit{Nothing objected}, because the
offending pairs had been SRDF-excluded and the offline check excluded the same
pairs by the same argument. \textbf{An exclusion silences the alarm; it does
not move the metal.}}

\dfct{The old mount position made 150\,mm of clearance impossible}
{At $(\pm0.15, -0.12)$ the base tube starts 0.010\,m from the torso box, so
over \emph{every} rotation the ceiling for \code{base\_link} was
$+0.0097$\,m --- infeasible by a factor of 15 before any rotation was chosen.
The mount was moved onto the back of the pack.}

\dfct{Clearance and reach are in direct conflict}
{Moving the mount back to gain clearance pushes the task volume toward the edge
of the arm's 0.902\,m reach: mount $y=-0.12$ gives 0.026\,m of clearance at
67\,\% of reach; $y=-0.32$ gives 0.211\,m at 88\,\%. Two of the seven mount
criteria (clearance $\ge$ 150\,mm, IK $\ge$ 80\,\%) cannot both be satisfied at
this scale, and the resolution is mechanical: a longer-reach arm, a task volume
closer to the body, or a mount raised above the shoulder.}

\dfct{The wearer model has static self-collisions}
{Head against torso 0.040\,m, hips against legs 0.050\,m --- two fixed links
overlapping by construction, nothing to do with the arms, and previously
reported as an arm problem.}

\dfct{The mount guard's first version cried wolf}
{Bounding spheres fired at 0.113\,m on a geometry with 0.166\,m of real
clearance. \textit{Recorded because the failure mode matters:} a guard that
cries wolf gets switched off, which is worse than no guard.}

\dfct{Four mechanisms could stop all motion silently}
{The blocking was usually correct; the defect was that blocking was
\emph{indistinguishable from working}. \textit{Fix:} every mechanism that can
stop motion registers with a \code{BlockMonitor} that is loud and by name on
the first block, escalates past 3\,s, and \emph{raises at registration} if the
blocker cannot state how it clears --- a blocker that cannot say how it clears
is a latch.}

\dfct{\code{ik\_inflight} latched active forever}
{The instrumentation's own bug: \code{clear()} sat on a line only reached when
\code{pending} was already False, which never happens on a healthy system. It
held for 65\,s reporting ``waiting 0.00\,s''.}

\dfct{\code{/estop\_reset} is a service, not a topic}
{The first injection run published a \code{Bool} at it, nothing listened, the
e-stop stayed latched, and \textbf{six subsequent faults reported NOT HANDLED
for that one reason}. Tests must reset to a known-good state between faults.}

\dfct{Channel auto-disable defaulted to off}
{\code{channel\_manager}'s auto-disable threshold was 0 frames, so a channel
could drop out for an entire trial with nothing marking it dead. Set to 25
frames --- 0.5\,s at 50\,Hz, the measured length of a real \code{j4} burst ---
with a required-channel dropout now publishing a trial ABORT.}

\dfct{The dead-man's enable was latched at construction}
{\code{estop\_node} read \code{deadman\_enabled} once, so setting it at runtime
did nothing --- and \code{participant\_safety\_node}'s attempt to force the
dead-man on for a participant session would have failed silently.}

\dfct{\code{ros2 param set} hangs on this machine}
{It goes through the daemon. Anything that shells out to it fails silently and
reports success it has not earned. All parameter changes go through a parameter
client, are read back, and are echoed \code{old -> new}.}

\dfct{Duplicate node instances corrupt the blocking view}
{Three stale followers published under the same unit key and overwrote each
other's state.}

\dfct{Six files used the wall clock to measure intervals}
{The same bug class that had produced a $-2321$\,ms latency reading, fixed once
in the bridge and left everywhere else --- including \code{sim\_to\_real\_bridge}
(the command path) and \code{real\_homing\_node} (which drives the real arm).
All converted to \code{time.monotonic()}; wall-clock \emph{timestamps}
deliberately kept.}

\dfct{The dead-man could not see the failure that actually happens}
{\code{master\_pose\_node} does not go silent when the master degrades: on a
validation failure it substitutes \code{last\_good} and keeps publishing at
50\,Hz --- and it stamped that substituted frame \code{now}. A dead-man keyed on
message \emph{arrival} therefore could not fire at all. \textbf{Only a clean
unplug produced silence, which is why every cable-pull test passed and the
mechanism looked correct for months.} \textit{Fix, at both ends:} the source
stamps the time of the \emph{data} it was built from; the dead-man keys on the
header stamp, and warns if a stamp is empty. Measured after: trips at
0.94--0.99\,s on a frozen-but-publishing master, 0.97\,s on a silent one, no
false positive over 243 fresh frames.}

\dfct{The e-stop halt was 4.0 seconds late}
{\code{trip()} called the real-driver halt first, which made two
\code{wait\_for\_service(timeout\_sec=2.0)} calls on a service that does not
exist on any simulation or mock stack --- inside the e-stop's own 10\,Hz timer
on a single-threaded executor. Measured: decision at 0.97\,s, trip logged at
4.98\,s. \textbf{Detection was never late; the halt was, and the log line that
would have revealed it was itself behind the block.} \textit{Fix:} park first,
publish \code{/estop\_state} first, and never wait for a service there.}

\dfct{An arm that had never published exempted itself from its own dead-man}
{\code{if t is None: continue}. Only the left master usually publishes, so the
right arm's dead-man was permanently disarmed.}

\dfct{A reset was measured against staleness that predated it}
{So a latch caused by a brief gap re-tripped on the next tick and could not be
cleared. Age is now measured from the later of ``last fresh data'' and
``armed/reset''.}

\dfct{The follower's e-stop block was invisible exactly when it mattered}
{The \code{estop} blocker was set and cleared only inside \code{on\_pose}, and
the commonest reason for the e-stop to latch is the dead-man --- which fires
\emph{because} the master went silent, so \code{on\_pose} stops being called at
the same instant. From the aggregated view the follower simply went quiet,
which is indistinguishable from a crashed node.}

\dfct{The latch pattern, and why grading instances was the wrong fix}
{Every blocker is written as \code{if <bad>: block(); return} followed by
\code{clear()}, so the clear sits on the fall-through path of the guard it
reports on --- fine while the guard is mutated elsewhere, and a latch the
moment the guard also stops the callback running. \textbf{It happened five
times, the fifth inside the code written to catch the first four.}
\textit{Structural fix:} auto-expiry of any blocker not re-asserted for 2.0\,s.}

\dfct{An expired blocker read as an all-clear}
{Expiry set \code{active=False}, and every \emph{consumer} read that as clear
--- \code{preflight}'s ``no active blockers'' check would have passed on a
crashed guard and let a participant session start. Expiry is now a third state:
\code{blocked} stays True, because ``nobody is asserting it'' is not evidence
the condition went away. \textit{Two placement rules are load-bearing:} the
follower's refusal sits at the publish rather than at callback entry (refusing
at entry would prevent the re-assertion that clears the unknown), and
\code{state\_unknown} is excluded from its own trigger or it re-arms itself
forever.}

\dfct{Four fault-injection harness bugs, each producing a table of clean runs while testing nothing}
{(1)~A fixed delay is not ``mid-trial'' --- trials run 0.4--2.5\,s with gaps, so
one offset landed inside a trial in some runs and in a gap in others.
(2)~The publisher must already be discovered: building the injector node after
the trigger costs about 1\,s of DDS matching, longer than a trial.
(3)~\code{/trial\_state} persisted across experiments, so the previous run's
final trial index still satisfied ``trial 3 is running'' and the fault fired
into the \emph{next} experiment's startup. (4)~\code{/participant/abort} is a
one-shot event, so a fault raised between two trials was seen by neither.
\textit{And the assertion must require the injected cause}, or an unrelated
standing fault invalidates a trial and scores as a pass for an injection that
never landed.}

\dfct{A wholly-invalid session analysed cleanly}
{Before \file{validity.py} existed, a session in which every trial was
invalidated analysed to ``0 valid trials'' followed by empty means and NaN
intervals --- indistinguishable from a null finding by anyone reading the
output. One shared definition of ``analysable'' now exits non-zero when no
trials survive or when more than half were excluded.}

\dfct{One experiment had no run or analyse scripts at all}
{E6 shipped with a config, a protocol and scenarios, and nothing to run. Found
by attempting to inject a fault into all six.}

\dfct{A fifth unprefixed resource, invisible to every URDF check}
{\code{tcp/twist.*} and \code{reset\_fault/*} are hardcoded string literals in
the driver C++, not in any xacro. \textit{And the gripper one is worse:} an
earlier fix prefixed the GPIO \emph{declaration} in the xacro, so afterwards
the URDF declared \code{left\_reactivate\_gripper} while the driver still
exported \code{reactivate\_gripper} --- they disagreed. It went unnoticed
because that driver is \code{COLCON\_IGNORE}d and \textbf{every gripper test to
date ran against \code{mock\_components}, which reads the URDF and never runs
that code}.}

\dfct{\code{arm:=right} would have opened a session against the left arm}
{\file{real\_arms\_highlevel.launch.py} passed \code{left\_robot\_ip}
unconditionally, so selecting the right arm would have connected to the left
one and homed it.}

\dfct{The two-arm start waited for one homing completion, not two}
{The first arm finishing ended the wait and enabled the bridge while the second
was still moving.}

\dfct{The homing ``stall'' was a wrong exit test}
{Completion required every joint inside the 1\dg{} deadband while the
acceptance criterion is 0.05\,rad (2.86\dg). A joint resting at 2.5\dg{} is
homed by the project's own definition and could never satisfy the exit test, so
homing ran on until the stall timer fired. \textbf{The deadband's job is to
suppress the correction, not to decide when the arm is home.}}

\dfct{Three more homing diagnoses that did not exist}
{Hysteresis was missing, so a joint would stop just inside the band, drift out,
get kicked and overshoot back in --- the observed 2.2--3.0\dg{} hunting.
OSCILLATING is now reported separately from STALLED, from error sign changes,
because ``stalled'' sends you looking for a seized joint on an arm that is
moving. And BELOW VELOCITY FLOOR is a third diagnosis: at 2.5\dg{} of error the
law commands 0.0218\,rad/s, and if the arm cannot execute that the error can
never close.}

\dfct{The FSR gripper node was in no launch file}
{It had only ever been started by hand, so a launched stack silently had no
grippers at all.}

\dfct{Three bugs in the trajectory-capture analyser, all found by known-answer testing}
{(1)~\code{C\_left\_up} matched the direction \code{left} before \code{up} ---
the arm names collide with the direction names, so every up/down segment was
filed under left. (2)~Correlation included dropout zeros, so a dropout-ridden
channel correlated with anything that moved: it was measuring the fault, not
crosstalk. (3)~The linearity $R^2$ was meaningless (0.000 for every healthy
channel) because it regressed against \emph{sample index}, measuring the shape
of the operator's sweep rather than the sensor.}

\dperiod{2026-08-06 --- the capture, and what it invalidated}{The recorder, the
channel verdicts, and a 35-minute capture that measured almost nothing.}

\dfct{The recorder oversampled the sensor, and that faked every per-row statistic}
{Rows at 50.3\,Hz against potentiometers updating at 14.7--17\,Hz, so
\textbf{82--88\,\% of adjacent rows are bit-identical}. Median $|\Delta|$ over
rows was 0.000 on all fourteen channels --- that is the recorder, not the
sensor; over distinct updates the real noise floor is 0.2--5.4\dg.
\textbf{The ``spread exactly 0.0 over 40 samples'' signature used for weeks to
identify a dead channel is this artefact}, not evidence.}

\dfct{Two priors that had been wrong for weeks}
{Left \code{j7} is the \emph{cleanest} channel on the left arm --- 0\,\%
dropouts, 0\,\% implausible jumps, merely stiff at 26\dg{} of range --- and had
been recorded as railed and dead. And half the left arm is INCOHERENT, which is
worse than had been documented.}

\dfct{INCOHERENT: the verdict that did not exist}
{A failing potentiometer does not go quiet. It returns values spanning a wide
range that are \emph{not a trajectory}: consecutive updates unrelated, jumping
tens of degrees in 60\,ms. Range alone calls that healthy, which is how a
broken channel keeps being trusted. The rule is now $>5$\,\% of updates jumping
$>60$\dg.}

\dfct{Range must be circular, or a 360\dg{} sensor reports 314\,363\dg}
{Unwrap-and-subtract is invalid for a rotary sensor: across a dropout gap the
unwrap cannot know how far the joint moved and the offset runs away. Range is
now the smallest arc containing every sample, bounded by 360 by construction.
Samples outside $[0,360]$ --- 0.2--0.8\,\% serial parse glitches, in bursts up
to 27 --- are rejected first.}

\dfct{The 35-minute capture was clutched out and mostly invalid}
{41 of 42 directional segments were recorded with the clutch \emph{disengaged}
on the arm under test, simulated end-effector span 0.000\,m.
\textit{Mechanism:} \code{GATE\_INDEX} mapped each arm to its \emph{own} clutch
button, so every gating press disengaged the clutch being measured ---
\textbf{and the comment directly above it stated the correct rule while the code
did the opposite}. The real arms also never moved (the CSV has no
\code{real\_*} columns at all), and the e-stop was latched for all of one block
because the both-button trigger fired on any two button \emph{edges} within
0.4\,s and gating taps supplied them.}

\dfct{The curses GUI crashed on launch, and its test passed on the broken code}
{It failed with no tty and under \code{TERM=dumb}, and the cleanup's own call
failed too and replaced the traceback. \textbf{The old test drove the program
through \code{script -qec}, which allocates a pty, and set
\code{TERM=xterm}} --- it constructed exactly the environment where the bug
cannot occur, then checked only for panel captions, never the exit code or a
traceback.}

\dfct{GUI control keys blocked the draw loop}
{For seconds at a time when a target node was absent, so the console appeared
hung exactly when it was being used to find out why something was hung.}

\dfct{The reachability measurement ran from the wrong pose}
{Both arms were parked 155--166\dg{} from home, left over from the capture, so
``reachability from home'' was never measured; the right arm reported 0.000\,m
in all 26 directions. \textit{Fix:} the tool now \emph{refuses to sample} unless
every joint is within 0.05\,rad of home, and prints the check. That assertion is
what was missing --- without it the tool happily measured the wrong question and
reported it under the right name.}

\dfct{Isotropy swinging between runs was the statistic, not the measurement}
{Two consecutive runs from an identical pose gave isotropy 0.28 then 0.12. At
$N=10$ the interquartile range is \textbf{0.000\,m on every direction, both
arms}. \textit{Mechanism:} isotropy computed as the minimum over directions of a
\emph{single} sweep is the statistic most sensitive to a rare tail failure. On
medians it is stable.}

\dfct{The IK ``sticking'' was compounding, and it was in the measuring tool}
{Per-call false-negative rate on a known-solvable target is 0.0--0.4\,\%,
sporadic, never the same direction twice. A sweep is a chain of up to 90 calls
and any one failure ends the direction early: at $p=0.002$ that is about 13\,\%
of directions, matching the 9-of-26 early terminations observed.
\code{ik\_follower\_node} never suffers it because it re-seeds and retries ---
so \textbf{the sweep was more brittle than the system it characterises}. Given
the follower's retry, early terminations went 9 to 0 and 3 to 0, and the medians
barely moved.}

\dfct{Raising the IK solver timeout does nothing}
{Requested timeouts of 5, 20, 50 and 100\,ms all returned a median solve time of
6.5--7.0\,ms. The request timeout is ignored on this path;
\code{kinematics.yaml} governs.}

\dperiod{2026-08-07 --- the task scenes, and the geometry that stopped a programme}{Two arms with no shared workspace, a mount sweep recorded and not applied, and the standing rule written down.}

\dfct{The two arms share no workspace at all}
{Every task target failed --- 0 of 19 --- with collisions off and with yaw
sampled, so it is reach and not scene collision. The decisive measurement is a
$7\times3\times3$ grid over the frontal volume: \textbf{0 of 63 cells reachable
by both arms}, 16 left-only (all at $x \ge +0.4$), 18 right-only (all at
$x \le -0.4$), and 29 by neither --- \textbf{including the entire centreline}.
\textit{This explains the operator's original symptom directly:} ``it does not
reach toward the centre'' is not a control fault; the centreline is reachable
by neither arm.}

\dfct{A correction to that framing, recorded because it was acted on}
