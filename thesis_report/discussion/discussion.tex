\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{What the geometry decides}

The most consequential outcome of this project is not a control result but a
geometric one: as mounted and as parked, the two arms share no reachable
workspace. That removes an entire class of task, and it does so in a way no
planning, control or autonomy change can address, because the constraint is on
where the arms can be at all rather than on how they get there.

It is worth stating precisely what follows and what does not. Tasks requiring
the two arms to meet at a point are impossible. Tasks requiring two distinct
points a fixed distance apart are not, and were shown feasible once the
coupled objects were widened to a \SI{500}{\milli\metre} span. The distinction
was initially missed, and a bimanual programme was briefly declared blocked on
the strength of a test that answered the stronger question. The correction
matters more than the original error: the requirement a task actually imposes
should be tested, rather than a convenient proxy for it.

That the fix is mechanical and quantified --- three to twenty-two shared cells
for a buildable mount change --- is what turns the null result into a
contribution rather than a complaint.

\section{Two constraints, one boundary}

Wearer clearance and top-down grasp feasibility were measured independently,
for different reasons, and both place the usable boundary at
$|x| \approx \SIrange{0.25}{0.30}{\metre}$. Neither measurement was designed to
corroborate the other, which is what makes the agreement informative: the
inboard band is unusable for two unrelated reasons at once.

The clearance half of that pair deserves emphasis because it is easy to
mistake for a tuning problem. The planner's collision check and the deployed
clearance floor ask different questions --- contact against margin --- and a
pose can satisfy the first while being refused by the second. Attempting to
resolve it by moving the working volume forward fails instructively: clearance
improves and reachability collapses, because the volume moves towards the edge
of the arm's reach as it moves away from the body. The axis along which the
conflict resolves is lateral, and that was not obvious in advance. It was
found only because the probe recorded \emph{which link} was closest rather
than only how close it was.

The ethical position is one-directional. The floor exists to protect a person
who did not choose the motion, so the task moves and the floor does not.

\section{Instrumentation as a first-class concern}

The pattern reported in \cref{sec:instrument} --- that surprising measurements
were more often instrument defects than system defects --- became the
methodological centre of the work. The response, requiring every analysis
routine to recover a constructed answer before being trusted with real data,
is in the author's assessment the most transferable outcome.

It is also incomplete, and honesty requires saying so. The rule was adopted
part-way through, and the earlier measurements have not all been re-derived
under it. Where a figure in this thesis originates in the engineering log
rather than in a retained artefact, it is marked as such.

\section{Silent failure as the dominant mode}

The safety work consistently found that the problem was not mechanisms failing
to act, but that acting and not acting looked identical from outside. The
dead-man defect is the clearest case: the mechanism was correct, its trigger
condition was correct, and it could not fire on the failure that actually
occurs, because the upstream node degraded by republishing stale data rather
than by falling silent. Every test of it had passed, because every test
removed the cable.

The general lesson is that a test exercising a failure mode different from the
one that occurs provides confidence without providing safety.

\section{The plan and the delivery}

The delivered system diverges substantially from the planning report, and the
divergences are engineering decisions rather than omissions.

\textbf{The simulator changed} from Isaac Lab to MuJoCo and then to ROS~2 with
MoveIt. Isaac Lab requires a heavyweight GPU stack; the development machine
carries \SI{4}{\gibi\byte} of video memory already committed to the perception
models. More importantly, the question that actually needed answering was
collision-aware kinematics against a wearer model, which MoveIt provides
natively.

\textbf{The compliance layer was not built.} The manufacturer's impedance mode
requires the cyclic control interface, and that interface is unusable on this
machine: measured write times of \SIrange{3}{6}{\second} against a
\SI{10}{\milli\second} budget, because the realtime channel is UDP and the
network translation layer breaks it. The delivered path uses the high-level
velocity API instead, which cannot express joint impedance. This is an
infrastructure constraint, not a design preference, and it is the reason the
haptic objectives could not be attempted.

\textbf{The master arms are passive.} This follows from the above: with no
force channel there is nothing for master actuators to render.

\section{Evaluation against the planning report's targets}

Of the nine targets in the planning report, none was met and none refuted.
Four are measurable with what exists; three require building a subsystem
first; one requires participants.

The three that require building are worth distinguishing carefully, because
calling them merely ``unmeasured'' would understate the position. Force
reflection and closed-loop overshoot are \textbf{structurally undefined on this
platform}: the mock hardware interface returns identically zero for every
force and effort field, so a number derived from it exists but carries no
information. Angular tracking is undefined for a different reason: the
commanded orientation is pinned to the anchor and never follows the master's
wrist, because the wrist channels are dead or incoherent.

\section{Limitations}

\subsection{Nothing was validated on the robot}

No result here was obtained from a physical Kinova arm or a participant. The
simulation uses a mock interface that echoes commands with no dynamics, so it
cannot answer questions about payload, compliance, latency under load, or any
consequence of real actuator behaviour. Where it has been asked such a
question it returned a null result that is a property of the mock rather than
evidence about the robot.

\subsection{The input is degraded and the baseline depends on it}

Seven of fourteen master channels are unreliable, and the degradation is
progressive: channels healthy in July were incoherent ten days later. Every
direct-teleoperation condition depends on those channels, so the baseline
against which autonomy would be compared is currently compromised. Repairing
two specific channels was identified by regression as the highest-value
intervention.

\subsection{The lateral axis is wrong and cannot be fixed by remapping}

Azimuth is derived from the shoulder roll joint alone. That joint's axis is
fixed in the mount, so rolling it moves the tip only once a downstream joint
has offset the tip from the roll axis; azimuth per degree of roll therefore
depends on how bent the arm is. Azimuth and elevation are consequently not
independent, which is precisely what the spherical model assumes. No axis
permutation can repair this --- it is a modelling error, not a sign error --- and
a hybrid using the full forward kinematics was tested and made the reversal
metric worse, because it inherits errors from four suspect joint angles rather
than one.

\subsection{The latency figure, when it exists, is a lower bound}

The end-to-end latency measurement designed for this system instruments the
control path from serial read to commanded joint state. Under the mock
interface the final stage measures command echo rather than physical motion,
so the resulting figure is \textbf{control-path latency and a lower bound} on
true end-to-end latency. That caveat travels with the number wherever it
appears.

\subsection{The experimental programme is designed but unexecuted}

Protocols, measures, counterbalancing and analysis routines exist and have
been exercised with scripted input. No participant has taken part, so every
hypothesis remains pre-registered intent.

\subsection{The version history cannot date the work}

The repository's commit history consists of one large import and a single day
of subsequent commits, so it cannot support claims about the order or timing
of development. The dated engineering log is the usable chronology and is a
laboratory notebook rather than an independent record.
