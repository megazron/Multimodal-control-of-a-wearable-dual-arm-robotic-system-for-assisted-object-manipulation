\section{Detection and grasp synthesis}

Detection is open-vocabulary (YOLO-World~\cite{cheng2024yoloworld} with
FastSAM~\cite{zhao2023fastsam}), falling back to a hue-and-depth filter; the
backend that produced a detection is reported with it. Range is used as a
discriminator where hue cannot be: the laboratory's teal robots and the
target green block differ in hue by only two units, well inside noise, but
sit \SI{2.8}{\metre} away against a \SI{1.6}{\metre} working range, so range
separates them at zero extra cost -- the same size-at-range expression
($w=sZ/f$) also rejects a \SI{210}{\milli\metre} pad presented as a
\SI{40}{\milli\metre} cube. Grasp synthesis takes the principal axes of the
object's depth cloud: writing the mean-centred points as $\mathbf{P}_k$, the
axes are the eigenvectors of the scatter matrix
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{P}_k-\bar{\mathbf{P}})(\mathbf{P}_k-\bar{\mathbf{P}})^{\!\top},
  \qquad \mathbf{C}\mathbf{u}_j=\lambda_j\mathbf{u}_j,\; \lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3,
  \label{eq:pca}
\end{equation}
and the jaw closes across $\mathbf{u}_3$, the direction of least extent,
capped at
\begin{equation}
  w = \max_k \mathbf{u}_3^{\!\top}\mathbf{P}_k - \min_k \mathbf{u}_3^{\!\top}\mathbf{P}_k
  \;\le\; w_{\max} = \SI{85}{\milli\metre}.
  \label{eq:graspwidth}
\end{equation}
A geometric method can \emph{name} the cause of a refusal (too wide, no
depth, too few points), which a learned alternative cannot and which also
would not fit beside the detector in \SI{4}{\gibi\byte} of video memory.

A reachability gate checking candidate grasps for reach and wearer clearance
was inoperative for the life of the module: it scored the clearance from the
whole kinematic chain including the immobile mount stub, which reads a
constant \SI{0.2202}{\metre} in every pose and so could never fail against
the \SI{0.15}{\metre} floor. It now reads the moving-chain clearance
(\SI{0.4031}{\metre} at home) and a regression test requires a named refusal
when aimed at the wearer's torso.

\section{Measuring the working area}

The sweep holds one wrist attitude per pass and translates, printer-bed-probe
style, rather than re-aiming at each grid point: re-aiming would make a
residual camera-to-wrist error a \emph{different} error in every frame,
which can neither cancel across views nor be estimated from them. A frame is
accepted only once no joint has moved by more than \SI{0.0015}{\radian} over
\SI{400}{\milli\second} -- a tenth of the arrival tolerance.

An earlier surface error of \SI{-5.2}{\milli\metre} was itself an instrument
defect (\cref{ch:instrument}): the reported height was the fitted plane's
intercept at the \emph{origin}, a point inside the wearer, not its height
over the working area. Reachability (a fact about the robot, computed once)
and the scene map (a fact about the room, rebuilt from scratch every time)
are separate products with separate lifetimes; \repo{scripts/pick\_from\_map.py}
refuses to move from a map older than thirty minutes, because a stale map is
indistinguishable from a fresh one by inspection.

\section{Planning and natural language}

\repo{joint\_planner.py} implements RRT-Connect~\cite{kuffner2000rrtconnect}
in joint space (the binding constraint is the whole arm's distance to the
wearer, not the hand), evaluated against the same moving-chain clearance the
guard enforces, with every edge densified at \SI{0.05}{\radian} to match the
path checker. Without a planner, a pick whose pre-grasp and grasp poses were
each individually clear passed within \SI{0.1462}{\metre} of the wearer
mid-path.

\section{Observing the wearer}
\label{sec:wearer-tracking}

The clearance floor of \cref{ch:methodsmain} is enforced against a body.
\repo{wearer\_tracker\_node.py} replaces the mannequin in the robot
description, part by part, with one built from markerless pose estimation
and metric landmarks lifted by perspective-n-point~\cite{lugaresi2019mediapipe}:
\SIrange{28}{34}{\milli\second} inference, \SI{61.6}{\milli\second} median
frame-to-estimate, \SIrange{62}{562}{\milli\second} end to end (the spread is
a configured publication rate limit, not the estimator). The safety
argument is one monotonicity rule enforced in one function: \textbf{the
camera may only ever make the wearer larger} -- per body part,
\texttt{fuse()} keeps whichever of the tracked and mannequin primitive is
\emph{nearer} the robot. A body injected \SI{0.10}{},\SI{0.30}{},\SI{1.00}{},
\SI{5.00}{\metre} further away moved the enforced margin by nothing; six
injected faults (dark frame, blank frame, two people, empty frame, occluded
arms, a control) were refused seven of seven, each naming the fault. The
honest limitation is on the fallback side: if the wearer raises their arms
and tracking is then lost, the guard reverts to arms-down and believes
\SI{0.317}{\metre} of clearance where there is \SI{0.093}{\metre}.

Before this was connected, the tracker reached the planning scene and
\emph{not} \repo{mount\_guard\_node}, which builds its wearer at import time
-- the body the planner avoided and the body the floor enforced against were
two different bodies. A second camera observing the workspace from outside
the arms is implemented, refuses to republish stale frames, and both
calibration tools pass self-tests to \SI{0.43}{\percent} focal length and
\SI{0.0000}{\metre}/\SI{0.000}{\degree} extrinsic error -- but \textbf{no
camera has ever been attached to it}: the device is the host's own webcam,
and forwarding it into the Linux environment needs an administrator-level
bind that was refused.
