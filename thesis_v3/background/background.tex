\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

Five bodies of work bear on the system built here.

\textbf{Supernumerary robotic limbs.} Parietti and Asada's founding
demonstrations brace a limb against the environment to stabilise the
wearer's posture~\cite{parietti2014bracing,parietti2013dynamic}, which turns
the wearer's body from a disturbance source into part of a closed chain.
Later reviews map the design space along consistent axes -- mounting
location trades reach against the moment arm imposed on the wearer,
actuation trades bandwidth against worn mass -- and note that the field
reports embodiment far more often than task
performance~\cite{yang2021srlreview,prattichizzo2021augmentation,zheng2026quantitative}.
Worn mass binds hardest: each Gen3 masses \SI{8.2}{\kilo\gram}, so the pair
with harness exceeds \SI{17}{\kilo\gram}, and simulated wearer loads suggest
the biomechanical cost is not negligible~\cite{moon2024impact}.

\textbf{The operator and the wearer as different people.} Fusion mounts a
robotic head and two arms on a worn frame, driven by a remote
operator~\cite{saraiji2018fusion} -- but its three modes are levels of
\emph{physical coupling}, not machine autonomy, and the publication reports
no user study. Work on near-body interaction found that raising
\emph{apparent} autonomy (a concealed operator, a wearer with no task of
their own) raised physiological arousal and lowered self-reported
trust~\cite{zhou2026proxemics}: the wearer's experience is not a free
variable, though this is not evidence about real autonomy or an occupied
wearer.

\textbf{Master interfaces for dissimilar kinematics.} Guggenheim and Asada
argue for miniature physical masters on the grounds that geometric
similarity supplies feedback inherently~\cite{guggenheim2021haptic}; the
argument covers similarity of \emph{form}, not \emph{scale}, and this
system's master-to-slave reach ratio is roughly one to three. Joint-space
correspondence preserves posture but needs every master joint measured
accurately; task-space correspondence decouples the chains at the cost of
the slave's redundant degree of freedom.

\textbf{Shared autonomy.} Dragan and Srinivasa formalise arbitration as
policy blending, weighted by prediction confidence~\cite{dragan2013policyblending};
Javdani et al.\ recast it as a POMDP over the user's goal and show that
assistance which does not wait for high confidence helps operators finish
faster~\cite{javdani2015hindsight}. Neither was developed for a robot mounted
on a second person, where the cost of assisting toward the wrong goal is not
a slower trial but a manipulator moving toward someone who did not command
it.

\textbf{Measuring embodiment, workload and trust.} Ownership over an
artificial limb is measurable and decomposes into ownership, agency and
location, which can move independently~\cite{botvinick1998rubber,longo2008embodiment}.
Workload separates into the NASA Task Load Index's six
subscales~\cite{hart1988nasatlx}; physical exertion is better tracked with a
Borg scale~\cite{borg1982perceived}, appropriate for an uncompensated master
arm or a carried mass.

\section{The gap}

No study measures the operator and the wearer together, under the same
autonomy, in the same trial: shared autonomy has been shown to help an
operator where the robot is attached to nobody, and raising apparent
autonomy has been shown to lower a wearer's trust where the autonomy was a
concealed human. Until both are measured in one system, the exchange rate
between what shared autonomy buys the operator and what it costs the person
carrying the arms is unknown. \Cref{ch:intro} states the aim this gap sets;
the full review, with every citation's scope stated precisely, is in
\cref{app:background}.
