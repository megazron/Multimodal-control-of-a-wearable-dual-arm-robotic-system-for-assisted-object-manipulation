# Pilot recordings gallery

Generated by `make_pilot_gallery.py` from the 23 VR sessions (P1–P5) and 9 master-arm sessions (M1–M3). Every figure is saved as `.pdf` (for LaTeX) and `.png` (for viewing). Verdicts: MAIN = would improve the graded main body; APPENDIX = worth having, uncounted; SKIP = made, says nothing new.

Sanity check run first: `vr_*_grip` equals `vr_*_engaged` in 99.84% of all samples and `grip_cmd_*` is empty in every session — the recorded 'grip' is the clutch button, and the gripper was never commanded in the pilot.

## `04_lag.pdf` — How far behind the operator the robot ran

- **Data:** trail.csv vr_*_lag_m while vr_*_engaged == 1; controller speed from vrc_*_x/y/z
- **Takeaway:** Lag is a few millimetres almost always and grows with hand speed; the fraction of samples past the 30 mm grasp gate is 0.5% (direct) and 0.9% (with assistance).
- **Numbers:** median lag 1.6 / 1.7 mm; >30 mm in 0.5% / 0.9% of samples
- **Suggested use:** MAIN

## `05_clutch_behaviour.pdf` — How operators used the clutch

- **Data:** events.jsonl clutch / clutch_refused events, active hand, every VR session
- **Takeaway:** With assistance operators re-engaged the clutch less often per minute and held it for longer stretches.
- **Numbers:** median engagements/min 6.6 vs 6.6; median hold 3.8 vs 4.3 s; refused 3.8 vs 3.8 per session
- **Suggested use:** MAIN

## `06_grip_is_the_clutch.pdf` — The 'grip' signal is the clutch button, not the gripper

- **Data:** trail.csv vr_*_grip vs vr_*_engaged; grip_cmd_* is empty in every session
- **Takeaway:** In every session the recorded grip signal is the same button as the clutch (99.8% of samples identical), and the gripper command column is empty: the gripper was never commanded in the pilot.
- **Numbers:** 99.8% agreement; grip_cmd_* empty in 23/23
- **Suggested use:** SKIP (but changes a caption: 'picked/placed' markers are clutch events)

## `08_sim_real_heatmap.pdf` — How closely the real arm followed the simulated one, joint by joint

- **Data:** trail.csv / trail_extracted.csv sim_*_jN vs real_*_jN, every session with both
- **Takeaway:** Almost every session tracks within a few degrees on every joint; the three M1 sessions stand out on several joints at once, the signature of a bad master channel rather than a bad joint.
- **Numbers:** 32 sessions; median per-joint error 1.44°, max 35.2°
- **Suggested use:** MAIN

## `08_sim_real_error.pdf` — Tracking error per joint, and does it grow with speed?

- **Data:** VR cohort sim_*_jN vs real_*_jN, pooled
- **Takeaway:** Errors are centred on zero for every joint and grow with joint speed: the real arm lags a moving command rather than sitting off a still one.
- **Numbers:** pooled 725991 samples; median |error| 0.13° at rest vs 2.59° in the fastest bin
- **Suggested use:** APPENDIX

## `09_master_raw_channels.pdf` — The master arm's raw sensor channels, a good session beside a bad one

- **Data:** trail_extracted.csv mraw_<hand>_p1..p7 for one M2 and one M1 object-tracking session
- **Takeaway:** Channels that trace a trajectory are alive; a channel that sits flat or jumps between levels is the defect the health verdict names.
- **Numbers:** M2 rms 2.2° vs M1 21.2°
- **Suggested use:** APPENDIX

## `11_paired_slopes.pdf` — Same operator, same task, both ways round

- **Data:** vr_study_sessions.json, the 7 participant×task pairs with both conditions
- **Takeaway:** The robot's hand travels further with assistance in 6 of 7 pairs, but the operator's own hand does not — so the extra travel is the assistance layer's, not the operator's.
- **Numbers:** robot path up in 5/7; operator's hand path up in 3/7
- **Suggested use:** MAIN

## `12_robot_vs_hand_travel.pdf` — How much the robot moved for each metre the operator's hand moved

- **Data:** vr_study_sessions.json ee_path_m and controller_path_m
- **Takeaway:** The operator's hand covers 10–30 m per session (raw controller motion, jitter included) while the robot's hand covers 0.2–7 m; per metre of hand motion the robot moves further with assistance.
- **Numbers:** median ratio 0.083 (direct) vs 0.084 (assistance)
- **Suggested use:** APPENDIX

## `12_robot_speed.pdf` — How fast the robot's hand moved while the operator was steering

- **Data:** trail.csv ee_*_x/y/z speed while vr_*_engaged == 1 and the hand was moving, pooled per condition
- **Takeaway:** The robot's hand moves at similar speeds under both conditions; assistance does not make it faster, it makes the path longer.
- **Numbers:** median 35 vs 45 mm/s
