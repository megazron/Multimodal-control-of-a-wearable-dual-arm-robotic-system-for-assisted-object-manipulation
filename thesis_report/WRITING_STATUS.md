Two of the CONFIRM markers are substantive rather than administrative and are
described in `EVIDENCE.md`:

- **`MPU6050.h` was not among the handed-over firmware files.** The interface
  the sketch calls is certain because the calls are in the sketch; the
  register sequence is as specified by the author and matches the device
  documentation, but the file itself must be attached.
- **The force channels.** The described design applies a floating-point
  exponential average so those channels carry decimal precision, and the
  ROS-side parser accepts a decimal in both fields. The delivered firmware
  transmits `%d` and applies no filter to them.

## Language conventions applied

No em dashes in body text. No "delve", "leverage", "furthermore", "moreover",
"in conclusion", "it is worth noting", and no "robust" used as filler. Past
tense for what was done, present for what is true. Findings stated before
they are interpreted.

## Maturity labelling

Every result carries one of three labels and the distinction is enforced
throughout: *measured on recorded hardware data*, *verified in simulation*,
*implemented*. **No result in this thesis was validated on a physical arm**,
and that sentence appears in the introduction, the results chapter and the
conclusion.

Two caveats travel with their numbers everywhere they appear:

- Anything derived from a force reading is **structurally undefined**, not
  merely unmeasured, because the mock hardware returns identically zero.
- Every latency is **control-path latency and a lower bound**, excluding the
  operator, the display and the physical arm.
