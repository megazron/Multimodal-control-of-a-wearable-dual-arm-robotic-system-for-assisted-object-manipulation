\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

\textbf{Supernumerary robotic limbs.} Parietti and Asada's founding
demonstrations brace an extra limb against the environment to steady the
wearer~\cite{parietti2014bracing,parietti2013dynamic}. Later reviews map the design choices along consistent axes -- where the
limb is mounted trades reach against leverage on the wearer, how it is
driven trades speed against carried weight -- and note that the field
reports how the limb \emph{feels} far more often than how well it
\emph{performs}~\cite{yang2021srlreview,prattichizzo2021augmentation,tong2021srlreview,zhang2023srlreview,zheng2026quantitative}.
Weight is the tightest limit: each Gen3 weighs \SI{8.2}{\kilo\gram}, the
pair with harness exceeds \SI{17}{\kilo\gram}, and simulated loads show a
real cost to the wearer's body~\cite{moon2024impact}.

\textbf{Operator and wearer as different people.} Fusion mounts a robotic
head and two arms on a worn frame driven by a remote
operator~\cite{saraiji2018fusion}, but its modes are degrees of
\emph{physical} coupling, not autonomy, and no user study is reported.
Raising \emph{apparent} autonomy -- a hidden human operator, a wearer with
nothing to do -- raised physiological arousal and lowered
trust~\cite{zhou2026proxemics}, so the wearer's experience matters. The
two-person case also differs mechanically: compensating a single wearer's
own body motion reaches \SI{1.37 \pm 0.58}{\milli\metre} of hand
error~\cite{zhang2024motioncomp}, but there the disturbance comes from the
operator's own nervous system, which knows what it is about to do; here it
comes from somebody else's.

\textbf{Controllers shaped unlike the robot.} Guggenheim and Asada argue
that a miniature controller of similar shape gives the operator an
intuitive feel for the robot~\cite{guggenheim2021haptic}; the argument
covers \emph{shape}, not \emph{size}, and this controller is a third the
robot's size. Copying joint angles needs every controller joint measured
accurately; copying hand position does not, but leaves the robot's spare
seventh joint undirected.

\textbf{Shared autonomy.} Dragan and Srinivasa formalise shared control as
a blend of what the operator commands and what the robot predicts, weighted
by the robot's confidence~\cite{dragan2013policyblending}; Javdani et al.\
treat it as a decision under uncertainty about the operator's goal and show
that assisting before the robot is certain still helps operators finish
faster~\cite{javdani2015hindsight}. Neither was developed for a robot on a
second person, where a wrong guess sends a manipulator toward someone who
did not ask.

\textbf{Measuring embodiment, workload and trust.} A person's sense that an
artificial limb is theirs is measurable and separates into ownership,
agency and location~\cite{botvinick1998rubber,longo2008embodiment};
workload is measured by the NASA Task Load Index~\cite{hart1988nasatlx},
exertion by a Borg scale~\cite{borg1982perceived}. Feedback that improves
control does not reliably improve ownership~\cite{pinardi2023feedback}, so
performance and embodiment must be measured separately; the pilot here
measures the first and claims nothing about the second.
