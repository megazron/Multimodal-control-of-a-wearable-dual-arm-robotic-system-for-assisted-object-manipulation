\chapter{Methods}
\label{ch:methodsmain}

This chapter summarises what was designed, built and run. Full engineering
detail is carried in \cref{ch:master,ch:method,ch:perception,ch:motion} so
it can be checked and reproduced without lengthening the argument here.

\section{The master arm}

The master is a miniature mannequin reproducing the Gen3's joint sequence at
roughly a third of its scale (\cref{fig:master-cad}, \cref{ch:master}), moved
with the fingers of one or both hands. A Teensy 4.1 reads fourteen rotary
potentiometers, two inertial units, two force-sensitive resistors and two
buttons, emitting one text frame at \SI{100}{\hertz} (\cref{tab:frame}).
Potentiometer counts are smoothed (\cref{eq:ema}) before conversion to
degrees; the inertial sensors are read as one burst so all six axes share
an instant.

Each channel carries a health verdict from its own recent history
(\emph{dead}, \emph{incoherent}, \emph{intermittent}, \emph{alive};
\cref{sec:channelhealth}), validation is scoped to the channels the active
mode consumes, and when no well-conditioned channel remains the system
reports position \emph{unavailable} rather than extrapolating one.

The commanded position is built in spherical form about the master's
shoulder rather than by forward kinematics from seven joint angles
(\cref{eq:elev,eq:azim,eq:reach}): elevation from the gravity vector,
azimuth from the shoulder roll, and radius from the forward-kinematic
magnitude of the bend joints, so a dead roll channel passes through as zero
without corrupting the radius. The commanded end-effector position is
an anchor plus a scaled displacement from a reference latched at clutch
engagement (\cref{eq:anchor}), so the clutch indexes like a mouse lifted
and set down, and reach is bounded by the robot rather than the master.

Five modes are delivered (\cref{tab:modes-main}); one safety stack serves all
of them, and dropping any shared mechanism is refused regardless of mode.
Shared autonomy arbitrates by threshold, not by continuous
blending~\cite{dragan2013policyblending} (\cref{fig:arbitration}): below a
confidence $\theta$ the operator's command passes untouched, above it the
robot supplies wrist orientation only, and position is never blended --
because a blend toward a wrong goal here moves metal toward somebody who
did not command it. The
operator sits across the room holding the VR controllers as a
six-degree-of-freedom input and watching the robot directly
(\cref{sec:desk}); the operator-to-robot alignment is a measured yaw, never
a mirror, since facing someone and copying them is a reflection that inverts
every orientation while positions look correct.

\subsection{Safety architecture}

Collision avoidance is graduated in three stages (\cref{sec:clearance}):
collision-aware inverse kinematics, redundancy re-seeding on rejection, and
a geometric clearance floor of \SI{150}{\milli\metre} measured from the
transform tree -- not the semantic collision description, which excludes
the proximal pairs a shoulder mount actually threatens. Every mechanism
able to stop the arm registers by name, must declare how it clears, and
expires into an explicit \emph{unknown} state (still blocking) if its own
loop stops running, so a blocker cannot silently keep a stale "clear."
Three independent triggers latch the emergency stop until reset: an
explicit call, both master buttons held together, and a dead-man keyed on
the master data's \emph{source} timestamp rather than message arrival,
since a degraded master keeps publishing stale values rather than falling
silent.

\section{Perception, planning and motion}

A calibrate-then-plan stage (\cref{ch:perception}) replaced declared object
and surface coordinates with measured ones: a wrist-camera sweep, one
attitude per pass, fits the working surface, segments objects from the image
and hands the result to an RRT-Connect planner \cite{kuffner2000rrtconnect}
checked against the safety layer's own clearance. Detection is open-vocabulary
(YOLO-World~\cite{cheng2024yoloworld} with FastSAM~\cite{zhao2023fastsam}),
falling back to a hue-and-depth filter -- a fallback that is load-bearing:
on real RGB-D (432 instances) the open-vocabulary detector found
\SI{9.0}{\percent}, a recall failure the geometric path does not share;
grasp synthesis is the geometric
principal-axis method of \cref{eq:pca,eq:graspwidth} rather than a learned
one, chosen because it can \emph{name} a refusal's cause and fits the
\SI{4}{\gibi\byte} of video memory on this machine.

Motion generation is Ruckig~\cite{berscheid2021ruckig}, a jerk-limited
generator driving every joint from a single scalar phase
(\cref{eq:phase-sync}) so the commanded configuration is on the line between
endpoints by construction, replacing a per-joint step limiter
(\cref{sec:clamp-maths}). The sim-to-real gap is modelled by replaying
recorded joint commands onto the physical arm and testing two candidate
models -- a terminal offset and a proportional gain -- which are
distinguishable only at short travel, so the error is propagated through
the manipulator Jacobian to select between them (\cref{sec:sim-to-real}).

\section{Verification methodology}

Twenty-six times in this project a result that looked like a system failure
was a defect in the measuring tool (\cref{ch:instrument}, \cref{sec:validity}).
The practice applied throughout: every analysis script carries a
known-answer test, synthetic data only where its ground truth is
\emph{constructed} rather than rendered, and a result contradicting an
earlier measurement is an instrument check until reconciled. Because the
solver (TRAC-IK~\cite{beeson2015tracik}) restarts randomly, every
reachability claim here is ten repeats over
a path densified to \SI{20}{\milli\metre}, and a pose not solid on all ten
is not reported as reachable. The perception pipeline applies the same rule
at each of its thirty-three stages: a result, a named refusal, or not
applicable -- never a silent zero (\cref{fig:pipeline-status}).

\section{Ethics and the pilot study}
\label{sec:ethics}

\textbf{This study was approved by Imperial College London's Science,
Engineering and Technology Research Ethics Committee, approval number
7111986.} All participants gave informed consent before taking part.
Participant data are anonymised throughout as P1--P5 and M1--M3;
\repo{write\_manifest()} refuses to record a name, email, date of birth,
address or telephone number, so anonymity is enforced in code, not only in
this text.

The full two-person protocol (\cref{app:study}) crosses direct teleoperation
against shared autonomy over five tasks with a separate wearer; it was not
run, because it depended on master channels that were incoherent and on an
approval that did not yet exist. Approval was then obtained and VR
controllers (\cref{tab:modes-main}) supplied a command independent of the
damaged channels, making a smaller, operator-only pilot practical first.

Five operators (P1--P5) each completed between two and seven recorded
sessions, performing manipulation-style tasks -- object tracking, target
reaching, position matching -- once under direct VR control and once with
shared-autonomy assistance, using the desk arrangement of \cref{sec:desk}.
Coverage is uneven, which \cref{sec:pilot-results} reports rather than
hides. Every sample was logged automatically at \SI{20}{\hertz} -- pose,
clutch engagement, VR spatial lag -- with an event log of every clutch and
emergency-stop transition, giving completion time, robot path, re-grips,
active-control fraction and lag with no manual timing. A further three
operators (M1--M3) attempted the same protocol from the mannequin master.
This pilot has no separate wearer, so H3--H5 remain for the session
\cref{app:study} specifies.
