\chapter{Experimental design}
\label{ch:study}

The system was built to make one measurement possible. This chapter specifies
that measurement completely: the question, the hypotheses, the design, the
instruments, the procedure and the analysis. None of it has been executed.
Setting it out in full is what makes \cref{ch:participant} an empty container
rather than an omission, and \cref{ch:discussion} states in priority order
what must be resolved before it can be run.

\section{The question}

Does supplying part of the operator's control with autonomy improve
performance, and what does it cost the person carrying the arms?

The second clause is the contribution. The shared-autonomy literature measures
the operator~\cite{dragan2013policyblending,javdani2015hindsight}; the
near-body interaction literature measures the
wearer~\cite{zhou2026proxemics}; no study measures both under the same
autonomy in the same trial, which is the gap identified in
\cref{ch:background}. Until that is done the exchange rate between them is
unknown, and an improvement of twenty per cent in completion time at the cost
of a point of wearer trust cannot be evaluated as a good trade or a bad one.

\section{Hypotheses}

Stated with direction, dependent variable and test before any data exists.

\begin{description}
  \item[H1.] Task completion time is shorter under shared autonomy than under
    direct teleoperation. \emph{Dependent variable:} completion time per
    trial. \emph{Test:} repeated-measures analysis of variance across mode
    within dyad, with a rank-based equivalent if the residuals fail
    inspection.
  \item[H2.] Operator workload is lower under shared autonomy, on the
    task-load index total and on the mental-demand subscale
    specifically~\cite{hart1988nasatlx}.
  \item[H3.] \textbf{Wearer-reported trust is lower under shared autonomy},
    against the direction of H1 and H2. This is the directional prediction
    taken from the concealed-operator study~\cite{zhou2026proxemics}, tested
    here with autonomy that is genuinely a program and a wearer who has a
    concurrent task of their own.
  \item[H4.] The wearer's sense of agency is lower under shared autonomy while
    the sense of ownership is unchanged, on the grounds that the two
    dissociate~\cite{longo2008embodiment} and only one of them has a
    functional referent when the operator is a different person.
  \item[H5.] The cross-arm interference coefficient in the dual-pursuit task
    is lower under shared autonomy, and the accompanying phase lag is not,
    which would identify the benefit as attentional rather than as a change in
    tracking delay.
\end{description}

H3 and H4 are the hypotheses worth running the study for. H1 and H2 are close
to assumed and exist principally to establish that the platform can show an
effect at all, which is what makes a null result on H3 or H4 interpretable
rather than merely quiet.

\section{Design}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/diagrams/experiment_design.tex}
  \caption[Experimental design]{Conditions crossed with tasks, together with
  the design options that were costed and rejected. Within-dyad throughout:
  between-subject variance on this task is larger than the effects sought.}
  \label{fig:expdesign}
\end{figure}

Two modes, direct teleoperation and shared autonomy, crossed with five tasks,
within subjects, at sixteen dyads. Roles are fixed for the whole session:
swapping them mid-session would halve the effective sample and confound role
with fatigue, and the wearer's role involves carrying \SI{17}{\kilo\gram},
which nobody should do twice in one visit.

\textbf{Two modes rather than four is a costed decision, not a convenience.}
Four modes crossed with five tasks is \SIrange{260}{320}{\minute} of task time
against a \SIrange{5.5}{6.5}{\hour} session, which exceeds by a wide margin
how long anybody should carry \SI{17}{\kilo\gram}. Three alternatives were
priced: two modes across five tasks at ten conditions and
\SI{167}{\minute}; four modes on a single task at eight conditions and
\SI{169}{\minute}, which still breaks the exposure cap; and a
between-subjects design across modes, which needs ninety-six participants to
reach the same sensitivity because between-subject variance on this task is
larger than the effect sought. The two-mode design is the only one of the four
that fits inside the wearer's exposure limit while retaining the task
contrast, and dropping the controller and full-autonomy modes is recorded as a
real loss of scope rather than absorbed silently.

Mode order is counterbalanced with Williams squares rather than a Latin
square, because a Latin square balances position but not immediate sequence,
and on a task with a learning curve the sequence effect is the one that bites.
At $n = 16$ the design achieves zero position imbalance and zero sequence
imbalance, verified by the counterbalancing module rather than assumed.

\subsection{Which cells exist}
\label{sec:cells}

One row of the design is unavailable, and it is a measured property of the
platform rather than a design decision.

\emph{Grasping under direct teleoperation is not achievable.} The default
orientation mode pins the commanded wrist to the anchor captured at the home
pose, the task-set block requires a rotation of \SI{169.7}{\degree} from that
anchor, and there is no master channel able to command it
(\cref{ch:results}). The recorded grasp demonstrations bypass the
teleoperation orientation lock by calling the solver directly, which
establishes that the \emph{robot} can execute an aligned grasp and not that an
\emph{operator} can command one.

\textbf{T2 therefore carries no mode contrast} and must be analysed as a
single condition. It is retained because grasp success and placement accuracy
under autonomy are worth measuring in their own right, and because it is the
one task where the categorical rather than quantitative advantage of autonomy
on this platform can be stated: the robot can execute an aligned grasp and an
operator cannot command one.

The coupled-carry tasks carry a second constraint that is geometric rather
than sensory. The object presents \SI{113.9}{\milli\metre} to the right
gripper along its closing axis against an \SI{85}{\milli\metre} jaw opening
(\cref{ch:results}), so the carrier must be re-specified to a grip the hand
can close on before those two rows can be run. The re-specification is
derived in \cref{ch:workspace} and the geometry is fixed; what remains is to
build it.

Both are stated in the design rather than in the limitations, so that the
empty cells in \cref{ch:participant} are not read as data that was lost.

\section{Tasks}

The first task is a positioning task with three target widths at each
amplitude, which characterises the interface against the speed-accuracy
relation~\cite{fitts1954information} and supplies the single-arm against
both-arms contrast that measures the attention bottleneck directly. It is the
only task available in every condition and therefore carries the main
comparison.

Exertion is taken from the wearer between every block rather than once at the
end. Fatigue under a carried load rises monotonically, so a single measurement
at the end converts a covariate into a guess. The task load index is
administered to \emph{both} participants, because the two roles do not load
the same way and the comparison between the two columns is the object of the
study.

The cross-arm interference coefficient requires a caveat that travels with it
everywhere it is reported. It is the total sensitivity of one arm's tracking
error to the other arm's target speed, and tracking error contains both an
attentional amplitude term and a lag term, since a fixed reaction delay $L$
becomes a positional error $vL$. The two are not separable from root-mean-square
error alone. The measured phase lag is therefore reported beside it: a large
coefficient with a flat phase lag is attentional, and both rising together is
not.

\section{Procedure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/diagrams/session_timeline.tex}
  \caption[Session timeline]{Session structure for two people. The wearer's
  exposure to the carried load is the binding constraint and is drawn as its
  own track.}
  \label{fig:timeline}
\end{figure}

Three people are present: the two participants and a safety observer whose
only task is to hold an emergency stop and watch the wearer rather than the
screen. That role exists because the operator is attending to the task and the
wearer cannot see behind their own shoulders. The observer's presence is
enforced in software as well as procedurally: the safety node treats two
seconds of silence from the observer's heartbeat as withdrawal of consent to
move, which is described in \cref{ch:method}.

Consent is taken separately and in separate rooms. The two participants may
know one another, and a wearer who feels observed by the person who recruited
them is not consenting freely.

The emergency stop is demonstrated and exercised with both participants
watching before any task begins, and the wearer is shown a stop within their
own reach.

\section{Analysis}

Comparisons are within dyad across mode. Where residuals permit, a
repeated-measures analysis of variance; otherwise its rank-based equivalent.
Post-hoc comparisons are Holm-corrected, and raw and adjusted values are
reported side by side so that the effect of the correction is visible.

Effect sizes are reported with confidence intervals, and the intervals rather
than the significance carry the interpretation. At sixteen dyads the design is powered for a large effect on completion time
and is not powered for a small one; that is stated in advance rather than
discovered afterwards. The effect sizes used in the power calculation are
estimates and are labelled as such: they must be replaced with pilot values
before the design is locked.

Trial validity is defined in one module shared by every analysis
(\repo{srl\_experiments/validity.py}) so that the definition cannot drift
between experiments. It excludes on the recorded validity flag, prints every
exclusion cause, and \textbf{exits non-zero when no trials survive or when
more than half were excluded}. Before that existed, a session in which every
trial had been invalidated analysed cleanly to zero valid trials followed by
empty means and undefined intervals, which is indistinguishable from a null
result to anyone reading the output.

\section{Ethics and risk}

The application is drafted and has not been submitted. A study cannot honestly
be submitted for approval while its baseline condition depends on sensor
channels that do not work.

The dominant risk is stated first: a \SI{17}{\kilo\gram} manipulator pair
mounted behind a seated person, moving under partly autonomous control, within
reach of their head and torso. Everything below follows from it.

anonymous, and no measure in \cref{tab:measures} requires one.
