\section{Architecture}

The system is organised as eight project packages under ROS~2 Jazzy, running
on Ubuntu 24.04 under WSL2. The dependency direction is one-way and
deliberate: \repo{srl\_teleop} contains teleoperation only and depends on
nothing else in the workspace, \repo{srl\_perception} adds detection,
\repo{srl\_autonomy} depends on both, and \repo{srl\_experiments} depends on
all of them.

That direction is load-bearing rather than tidy. Direct teleoperation is the
baseline against which every autonomy condition is compared, so if the
teleoperation package imported from the autonomy package the comparison would
be circular. The property was verified two ways: by search, and by physically
removing the autonomy and perception packages from the install tree and
confirming that the follower still reached its running state.

\section{The master arm}

The operator commands the system through a miniature mannequin: a scaled
replica of the Gen3 kinematic chain instrumented with single-turn
potentiometers and an inertial measurement unit, sampled by a Teensy~4.1 and
streamed over USB serial. Link lengths are
$\ell = [43, 37, 43, 37, 43, 36, 33]\,\si{\milli\metre}$
(\repo{master\_calibration.py}), summing to a full extension of
$L_{m} = \SI{0.272}{\metre}$ against the Gen3's \SI{0.902}{\metre} reach, a
size ratio of \num{3.32}. Joints alternate roll and bend, matching the Gen3
morphology.

\subsection{Serial protocol}

Frames are newline-terminated ASCII at \num{115200}~baud:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Master frame format, one line per sample.},
                   label={lst:frame}]
k1j1:<deg> ... k1j7:<deg> K1IMU:ax,ay,az,gx,gy,gz
k2j1:<deg> ... k2j7:<deg> K2IMU:ax,ay,az,gx,gy,gz
fsr1:<n>,fsr2:<n>,btn1:<0|1>,btn2:<0|1>
\end{lstlisting}

Frames arrive at approximately \SI{50}{\hertz} but the potentiometers update
at only \SIrange{14.7}{17}{\hertz}, so \SIrange{82}{88}{\percent} of adjacent
frames are bit-identical. That aliasing is not a curiosity: any statistic
computed over consecutive rows is dominated by duplicates, and it invalidated
an earlier noise-floor measurement (\cref{ch:results}).

The port is discovered rather than configured, because the board enumerates as
either \texttt{/dev/ttyACM0} or \texttt{/dev/ttyACM1} depending on attachment
order. Access is made exclusive with an advisory lock on the open descriptor.
This is not merely defensive: the port \emph{search} itself reads bytes, so
probing a port another node owns removes frames from that node's stream.

\subsection{Pose decomposition}

The commanded position is built in spherical form about the master's shoulder
rather than by forward kinematics of the full chain, because the full solution
collapsed directionally when evaluated against recorded gestures. Writing
$e$ for elevation, $a$ for azimuth and $r$ for reach,

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{m} = r\big(\cos e \cos a,\; \cos e \sin a,\; \sin e\big),
  \label{eq:spherical}
\end{equation}

with elevation taken from gravity alone,

\begin{equation}
  e = \arcsin\!\big(\hat{\mathbf{a}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}\big),
  \qquad \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{a}_{\text{meas}}}{\lVert\mathbf{a}_{\text{meas}}\rVert},
  \label{eq:elevation}
\end{equation}

where $\hat{\mathbf{a}}$ is the arm's long axis in the sensor frame, captured
once with the arm hanging, and $\mathbf{a}_{\text{meas}}$ is the measured
specific force. Because an accelerometer at rest reports specific force
pointing upward, $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ is world-up expressed in the sensor frame,
and \cref{eq:elevation} needs no mount rotation and cannot drift. Azimuth $a$
comes from the shoulder roll potentiometer alone, zero-referenced; reach $r$
is the magnitude of the forward kinematics of the proximal joints.

\subsection{A convention error worth recording}

The specification called for both $\hat{\mathbf{a}} = -\hat{\mathbf{a}}_{\text{rest}}$
\emph{and} a leading negation in \cref{eq:elevation}. The two compound: together
they place the hanging rest pose at $+90\si{\degree}$, i.e.\ straight up. Since
$\cos$ is even and only $\sin$ carries the sign, the effect is to mirror the
$z$ axis alone --- the symptom being that ``down'' commanded a higher pose than
``up''. Only one negation is correct, and the rest pose is asserted at capture
time as a self-check.

\section{Master-to-slave mapping}

The commanded end-effector position is a scaled displacement about a latched
reference:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{\text{cmd}} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{anchor}}
    + s\big(\mathbf{p}_{m} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{ref}}\big),
  \label{eq:mapping}
\end{equation}

where $s$ is the per-arm scale and $\mathbf{p}_{\text{ref}}$ is latched on the
first valid frame and re-latched on every clutch engagement. The scale is
\num{1.0}, chosen from measured reachability rather than from geometry: at
unity the commanded span is \SI{0.44}{\metre} with \SI{92}{\percent} and
\SI{95}{\percent} of recorded poses solvable for the left and right arms, and a
one-to-one mapping is the most predictable for an operator.

Latching the reference matters more than it appears to. An earlier version
referenced the master's own frame origin, so the first command after start-up
was a step of the master's entire tip vector, and the operator had to hold a
calibrated neutral pose for the system to be safe to start.

\section{Coordinate conventions}

The manufacturer's controller reports joint positions in degrees over
$[0,360)$; the middleware uses radians over $(-\pi,\pi]$. Writing $g$ for the
conversion,

\begin{align}
  \mathrm{wrap}_{180}(d) &= 180 - \big((180 - d) \bmod 360\big), \\
  \mathrm{wrap}_{\pi}(\theta) &= \pi - \big((\pi - \theta) \bmod 2\pi\big),
  \label{eq:wrap}
\end{align}

and the ordering is the substance: the controller value is wrapped into
$(-180,180]$ \emph{and then} converted, never the reverse. Motion between two
poses uses a signed shortest difference,
$\Delta\theta = \mathrm{wrap}_{\pi}(\theta_{t} - \theta_{c})$, applied only to
the continuous joints $\{1,3,5,7\}$; the limited joints use plain subtraction,
because for them wrapping would mean driving through a hard stop.

The failure mode justifies the isolation: an angle of \SI{350}{\degree} read as
$+350$ rather than $-10$ commands very nearly a full revolution of a joint
standing beside a person. The conversion is therefore confined to one module,
\repo{kortex\_convention.py}, and unit-tested at the seams.

\section{Calibration}

Three quantities are captured and stored per arm. The \emph{potentiometer
zero} is a per-channel offset and sign, applied as a relative reference so the
reading has meaning independent of assembly. The \emph{IMU reference}
$\hat{\mathbf{a}}$ is captured once with the arm hanging. The \emph{gyroscope
bias} is the mean of a settled rest sample, flagged stale if the residual rate
exceeds \SI{1}{\degree\per\second}.

A trap worth recording: the bias must be captured from a settled stream.
Reopening the serial port resets the microcontroller, and the IMU emits
nonsense while settling; a bias captured that way read
\SI{27.9}{\degree\per\second} of noise where the settled figure was
\SI{0.11}{\degree\per\second}.

The \emph{workspace anchor} is the arm's end-effector pose at its home
configuration, read from the transform tree rather than assumed. Because the
anchor is derived from the home pose, any change to the mount or to the home
angles invalidates it.

\section{Motion generation and safety}

Commanded poses are solved with TRAC-IK through MoveIt's inverse-kinematics
service with collision checking enabled against a model that includes the
wearer. Two mechanisms handle failure. On an unsolved pose the redundant
degree of freedom is re-seeded and the solve retried, fanning the elbow either
side of its natural posture. On a solution far from the current state the
follower commits a bounded step towards it rather than discarding it:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q}_{k+1} = \mathbf{q}_{k}
    + \mathrm{clip}\big(\mathbf{q}^{*} - \mathbf{q}_{k},\;
                        -\Delta_{\max},\; +\Delta_{\max}\big),
  \label{eq:slew}
\end{equation}

which converges in $\lceil d / \Delta_{\max}\rceil$ cycles. The distinction is
not cosmetic: an earlier implementation \emph{discarded} any solution further
than the threshold, which can never converge, because a discarded solution
leaves the arm where it was and the next solution is equally far away.

\subsection{Clearance}

Separately from the binary collision check, a geometric clearance is computed
from the transform tree between the arm's distal links and primitive
approximations of the wearer's body, giving a margin rather than a contact
flag. The follower refuses to publish below a configurable floor:
\SI{0.05}{\metre} in simulation and \SI{0.12}{\metre} in the real-robot
configuration. \Cref{ch:workspace} shows that this distinction --- contact
versus margin --- has substantial consequences.

\subsection{Emergency stop and dead-man}

Three independent triggers latch until an explicit reset: a service call, both
master buttons held simultaneously, and a dead-man on the master stream. On
trip, each arm's measured joint positions are republished as a short
trajectory, so a follower that continues commanding is continuously
overridden.

The dead-man keys on the \emph{source timestamp} of the master pose, not on
message arrival, and this is the single most important detail in the safety
layer. The master node does not fall silent when it degrades: it substitutes
its last good value and continues publishing at full rate. An arrival-keyed
dead-man therefore cannot fire on the failure that actually occurs, and only a
clean cable removal produces silence --- which is why every cable-pull test had
passed.

\section{Verification methodology}

Because much of this work consists of measurements, the method by which a
measurement is trusted is itself part of the method. Two rules were adopted
after repeated failures.

First, \emph{a pose that passes a single inverse-kinematics call is not a
reachable pose}. TRAC-IK restarts randomly, so a pose at the edge of the
feasible set is a coin flip. One pose measured \SIrange{72}{82}{\percent}
feasible per call, which passes a five-repeat check roughly one time in five.
Verification is therefore $N=10$ repeats over a path densified to
\SI{20}{\milli\metre}, because the arm flies the segments between declared
waypoints and those are not reachable by virtue of their endpoints being
reachable.

Second, \emph{an instrument must recover a known answer before a surprising
measurement taken with it is reported}. Every analysis routine that will touch
experimental data has a test that feeds it an input whose answer is known by
construction.
