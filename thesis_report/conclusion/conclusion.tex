\chapter{Conclusion}

This project produced a complete, safety-instrumented teleoperation stack for
a backpack-mounted pair of seven-degree-of-freedom arms commanded by a remote
operator, together with a characterisation of what that configuration can do.

The engineering contributions are the pipeline itself --- master-arm sensing
with a spherical pose decomposition, collision-aware inverse kinematics with
redundancy resolution, a clearance margin referenced to the wearer's body, and
a multi-path emergency stop whose blocking behaviour is made externally
visible --- together with a degraded-operation mode that keeps the system
usable on a subset of its intended sensing and refuses to invent a position
when none survives.

The measured contributions are a workspace characterisation of the mounted
configuration, including the negative result that the two arms share no
reachable workspace and that inter-arm handover is therefore blocked by
geometry; a quantified account of what degraded sensing costs; the finding
that the deployed clearance margin and the planner's collision test disagree
across the entire working band, and that the conflict is resolvable laterally
but not fore-and-aft; and a set of eighty-seven recorded verification runs
with automated checking of object handling, gripper actuation and wearer
clearance.

The methodological contribution is the discipline of validating an instrument
against a known answer before reporting a surprising measurement taken with
it. This arose from necessity rather than principle: across the project,
unexpected results were more often defects in measurement than in the system,
and the practice of requiring every analysis routine to recover a constructed
answer before being trusted with real data caught a substantial number of
errors that would otherwise have entered the record.

\section{Future work}

The immediate next steps are determined by the limitations rather than by
ambition. Two specific master-arm channels should be repaired, since the
analysis identifies them as the constraint on the left arm's usable motion and
every direct-teleoperation baseline depends on them. The clearance conflict
should be resolved by relocating the working band laterally, with the object
specifications re-derived and the scenarios re-verified in one deliberate
pass. The real-arm bring-up should be repeated with logging retained, so that
the control rate, homing behaviour and session handling become citable rather
than recorded only in a notebook.

Beyond that, the experimental programme is designed and instrumented but
unexecuted, and executing it is the substantial remaining work.
