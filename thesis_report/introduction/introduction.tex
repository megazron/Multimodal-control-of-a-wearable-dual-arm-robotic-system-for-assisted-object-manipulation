\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

Supernumerary robotic limbs (SRLs) are additional arms worn on the body which
act alongside the wearer's own limbs rather than replacing or rehabilitating
them. They are distinguished from prostheses in that the wearer retains full
natural function, and from fixed industrial manipulators in the single respect
that matters most for their control: the base of the robot is a person. It
accelerates, sways and steps; it is soft, valuable and irreplaceable; and it
cannot be commanded to hold still. \todo{CITE: an SRL review establishing the
taxonomy and the augmentation-versus-restoration distinction --- Tong et
al.~2021 [1] and Prattichizzo et al.~2021 [4] are both in the planning
report's bibliography}

The work reported in this thesis concerned the MUlti-limb Virtual Environment
(MUVE), known within Imperial College London as the Dr.\ Octopus platform,
which mounts two Kinova Gen3 seven-degree-of-freedom ultralight arms on a
backpack frame worn by an operator. \todo{CITE: the MUVE / Dr.~Octopus
platform page [2]} The specific problem addressed was the command interface:
how a person may direct two redundant seven-degree-of-freedom arms in a manner
that is intuitive enough to be usable without training, and safe enough to be
acceptable on a robot mounted beside a human head.

The approach taken, following the project's planning report, was a
\emph{miniature mannequin master}: a scaled physical replica of the Gen3
kinematic chain, instrumented with potentiometers and an inertial measurement
unit, which the operator manipulates with the fingers of one or both hands
while the full-scale arms follow. The appeal of this arrangement is that it
requires no abstraction on the operator's part. The master has the morphology
of the thing it commands, so the mapping from intention to motion is direct
rather than learned. \todo{CITE: Guggenheim and Asada~2021 [12] on miniature
physical masters and inherent haptic feedback}

A second architectural property emerged during the work and came to shape the
thesis. Because the arms are worn on a backpack, the natural deployment is one
in which the arms are borne by one person and commanded by another. That
configuration has a direct predecessor in the Fusion system, which mounted a
robotic head and two arms on a worn frame and drove them from a remote
operator. \todo{CITE: Saraiji et al., Fusion, SIGGRAPH 2018 Emerging
Technologies} It is also the configuration that makes the control problem
genuinely difficult, because the person issuing commands has neither
proprioception of the wearer nor any vestibular sense of the base motion
against which the arms must work.

\section{Objectives}

The objectives stated in the project's planning report were framed around a
haptic master--slave system with physical springs at the master and virtual
spring impedance at the slave. The delivered system diverged from that plan in
several respects, for reasons set out in \cref{ch:discussion} and analysed in
full in the accompanying divergence record. \todo{CONFIRM: whether the
divergence analysis should appear as an appendix in the submitted document or
remain a supporting file} The objectives below are the revised set, aligned to
what was built and measured.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item To design and fabricate a miniature instrumented mannequin master
    replicating the Gen3 morphology at reduced scale, and to characterise its
    sensing.
  \item To derive and implement the mapping from master pose to slave
    end-effector command, including the coordinate-convention conversion
    required between the manufacturer's controller and the robot middleware.
  \item To implement a safety architecture appropriate to a manipulator
    mounted beside a person, and to validate it by systematic fault injection
    rather than by inspection.
  \item To characterise the reachable workspace of the mounted configuration
    and to determine which manipulation tasks it can support.
  \item To establish a verification methodology yielding traceable evidence
    for every claim, and to apply it across the task set.
  \item To identify, by measurement, the constraints that must be resolved
    before hardware validation and a user study become possible.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Challenges}

Four difficulties consumed the majority of the effort, and each is treated at
length in the chapters that follow.

The first was \emph{kinematic dissimilarity between master and slave}. The
master replicates the Gen3's joint sequence but not its proportions: its links
sum to \SI{0.272}{\metre} against the full-scale arm's \SI{0.902}{\metre}
reach. A joint-to-joint mapping is therefore unavailable, and a mapping in
task space must contend with the fact that the two chains reach different sets
of poses. The resolution adopted was to abandon joint correspondence entirely
and map displacement of the master's tip onto displacement of the slave's end
effector about a re-latched anchor, which is described in \cref{ch:method}.

The second was \emph{a coordinate-convention mismatch}. The manufacturer's
controller reports joint positions in degrees over $[0, 360)$ while the
middleware uses radians over $(-\pi, \pi]$, and the two conventions must be
reconciled on every command. The failure mode is not a small error: an angle
of \SI{350}{\degree} interpreted as $+350$ rather than $-10$ commands very
nearly a full revolution of a joint standing next to a person. The conversion
was therefore isolated in a single module and tested at the seams where such
mistakes occur.

The third was \emph{calibration without power-on discontinuities}. The
potentiometers report absolute values whose meaning depends on how the master
was assembled, so a zero reference must be captured and stored. If that
reference is taken from a frame that has failed validation, or if the
commanded pose is referenced to the origin of the master's own frame rather
than to the operator's actual hand position, the first command issued after
start-up is a step of the master's entire tip vector.

The fourth, and the one that proved most pervasive, was that \emph{almost
every failure in this system is silent}. An arm that has stopped moving looks
identical whether it is blocked for a good reason, blocked for a bad one, or
receiving no data at all. A substantial part of the engineering effort went
into making internal state legible from outside, and a recurring finding of
the results is that the measuring instruments failed more often, and more
quietly, than the system being measured.

\section{Contributions}

The contributions claimed are limited to those the implementation and its
recorded artefacts can support. Each is stated with its maturity: whether it
was implemented, verified in simulation, or validated on physical hardware.
\textbf{No contribution in this thesis was validated on physical hardware},
for reasons given in \cref{ch:discussion}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A complete, safety-instrumented teleoperation stack} for a
    backpack-mounted pair of Gen3 arms, comprising master sensing, a spherical
    pose decomposition, collision-aware inverse kinematics with redundancy
    resolution, a geometric clearance margin referenced to the wearer's body,
    and a multi-path emergency stop whose blocking behaviour is externally
    observable. \emph{Implemented; verified in simulation.}

  \item \textbf{A coordinate-convention conversion layer} that isolates the
    degree-to-radian and wrapping problem in one auditable place, with a
    shortest-path joint difference that distinguishes continuous joints from
    limited ones. \emph{Implemented; unit-tested.}

  \item \textbf{A workspace characterisation of the mounted configuration},
    including the finding that the two arms share no reachable workspace under
    the as-built mount, which blocks inter-arm handover for geometric reasons
    that no control or planning change can address, together with a
    quantified and buildable mount modification that would resolve it.
    \emph{Verified in simulation;} \cref{ch:workspace}.

  \item \textbf{A quantified account of degraded-input teleoperation} ---
    which capabilities survive the loss of individual sensing channels, what
    each loss costs, and evidence of progressive hardware degradation between
    two dated recordings. \emph{Measured from recorded hardware data.}

  \item \textbf{A verification methodology} requiring that an instrument
    recover a known answer before a surprising measurement taken with it is
    reported, applied across a set of recorded runs covering every task,
    scenario and autonomy condition with automated checking of object
    handling, gripper actuation and wearer clearance. \emph{Applied
    throughout.}
\end{enumerate}

The fifth of these arose from necessity rather than principle. Across the
project, unexpected results proved more often to be defects in measurement
than in the system, and the discipline of validating an instrument before
believing its output caught a substantial number of errors that would
otherwise have entered the record. It is, in the author's assessment, the most
transferable outcome of the work.

\section{Thesis structure}

\Cref{ch:background} reviews supernumerary limb design, master--slave
teleoperation and the mapping problem between dissimilar morphologies.
\Cref{ch:method} describes the system as built, from the master hardware
through to the safety architecture. \Cref{ch:results} reports the measured
outcomes, organised by validation level and labelled throughout with the
strength of evidence behind each. \Cref{ch:workspace} presents the workspace
characterisation separately, because it is a chain of measurements whose
consequences determine the shape of the task set. \Cref{ch:discussion}
interprets these results, compares the delivered system against the planning
report's targets, and states the limitations honestly. \Cref{ch:conclusion}
restates what was achieved against the objectives and sets out the work that
would most improve the system.
