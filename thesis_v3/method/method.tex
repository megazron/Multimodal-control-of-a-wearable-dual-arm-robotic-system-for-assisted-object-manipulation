\section{The master arm}

The master is a miniature mannequin arm with the Gen3's joint layout at
roughly a third of its size (\cref{fig:master-cad}, \cref{ch:master}), moved
with the fingers. A Teensy 4.1 microcontroller reads fourteen rotary
position sensors (potentiometers), two inertial sensors, two
force-sensitive pads and two buttons, and reports at \SI{100}{\hertz}
(\cref{tab:frame}). Each reading $r_n$ is smoothed on the
microcontroller by an integer running average before conversion to degrees,
\begin{equation}
  e_{n+1} = \frac{\alpha\, r_n + (256-\alpha)\, e_n}{256}, \qquad \alpha = 64.
  \label{eq:ema-main}
\end{equation}
Each sensor carries a health
verdict from its own recent history (\emph{dead}, \emph{incoherent},
\emph{intermittent} or \emph{alive}; \cref{sec:channelhealth}); only the
sensors the active mode uses can reject a reading, and when no trustworthy
sensor remains the system reports the position as \emph{unavailable} rather
than guessing.

The commanded hand position is a direction and a distance from the master's
shoulder rather than a computation from all seven joint angles. With
$\mathbf{g}$ the measured gravity vector, $\hat{a}$ the upper arm's own axis,
$\theta_i$ the joint angles and $\theta_1^0$ the shoulder's rest angle,
\begin{equation}
  \phi = \arcsin\!\Bigl(\hat{a}\cdot\tfrac{\mathbf{g}}{\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert}\Bigr),\qquad
  \psi = \theta_1-\theta_1^0,\qquad
  r = \bigl\lVert \mathrm{FK}(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4,0,0,0)\bigr\rVert,
  \label{eq:map-main}
\end{equation}
so a dead rotation sensor zeroes $\psi$ without corrupting $r$. The robot's
target is a fixed reference plus a scaled displacement from where the master
tip $\mathbf{d}$ was when the operator pressed the \emph{clutch}, a button
held to make the robot follow:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{anchor}} + s\,(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{d}_{\text{ref}}),
  \label{eq:anchor-main}
\end{equation}
so releasing and re-pressing the clutch works like lifting and
repositioning a mouse.

Five modes are delivered (\cref{tab:modes-main}); one safety layer serves
all of them and no mode may switch any part of it off. Shared autonomy
decides by a threshold rather than by continuous
blending~\cite{dragan2013policyblending} (\cref{fig:arbitration}). With
$P$ the estimator's confidence in its guess of the operator's target and
$\mathbf{q}$ a wrist orientation,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{\text{cmd}} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{op}}\ \text{always};\qquad
  \mathbf{q}_{\text{cmd}} =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathbf{q}_{\text{op}} & P \le \theta \text{ or ambiguous},\\
    \mathrm{slerp}\bigl(\mathbf{q}_{\text{op}},\mathbf{q}_{\text{grasp}},\sigma(t)\bigr) & P > \theta,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:arb-main}
\end{equation}
blended in over \SI{0.5}{\second}. Position is never blended, because a
blend toward the wrong goal here moves the arm toward somebody who did not
ask. In the laboratory the operator sits across the room,
holding the VR controllers as a hand-position input and watching the robot
directly (\cref{sec:desk}); the operator-to-robot alignment is a measured
rotation, never a mirror image, since copying someone you face reverses
every orientation.

\subsection{Safety architecture}

Collision avoidance has three stages (\cref{sec:clearance}): the joint
solver rejects colliding poses, the spare joint is re-seeded and the solve
retried, and a clearance floor is enforced from where the arm actually is.
With $\ell$ ranging over the arm's links (modelled as tubes of radius
$r_{\text{tube}}$) and $w$ over the wearer's body parts, the arm holds
unless
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\ell,\,w}\ \bigl[\operatorname{dist}(\ell, w) - r_{\text{tube}}\bigr] \;\ge\; \SI{0.150}{\metre},
  \label{eq:floor-main}
\end{equation}
computed on the robot's own link model, not the planner's collision list,
which omits the shoulder-adjacent pairs. Every mechanism able to stop the arm
registers by name, states how it clears, and falls into an explicit
\emph{unknown} state -- still blocking -- if its process stops. Three
independent triggers latch the emergency stop until reset: a software call,
both master buttons held together, and a watchdog on the master data's
\emph{source} timestamp, because a failing master keeps sending stale values
rather than going silent.

\section{Perception, planning and motion}

A calibrate-then-plan stage (\cref{ch:perception}) replaced typed-in object
and table positions with measured ones: a wrist-camera sweep fits the table
surface, picks the objects out of the image and hands the result to a
motion planner (RRT-Connect~\cite{kuffner2000rrtconnect}) checked against
the safety layer's own clearance. Detection uses an open-vocabulary model
(YOLO-World~\cite{cheng2024yoloworld} with FastSAM~\cite{zhao2023fastsam})
with a colour-and-depth filter as fallback; the fallback carries the load,
since on real frames (432 object instances) the learned detector found
\SI{9.0}{\percent}. The table is the plane $(\mathbf{n}, d)$ on which most
depth points $\mathbf{P}_k$ lie, $|\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}_k + d| <
\SI{6}{\milli\metre}$, kept from 400 random draws~\cite{fischler1981ransac}.
Grasps come from each object's principal axes: with $\mathbf{u}_3$ the
direction of its least extent,
\begin{equation}
  w = \max_k \mathbf{u}_3^{\!\top}\mathbf{P}_k - \min_k \mathbf{u}_3^{\!\top}\mathbf{P}_k \;\le\; w_{\max} = \SI{85}{\milli\metre},
  \label{eq:grasp-main}
\end{equation}
the jaw closes across $\mathbf{u}_3$ and a wider object is refused by name
(\cref{eq:pca}) -- which a learned model cannot do.

Motion is generated by Ruckig~\cite{berscheid2021ruckig}, which limits
acceleration and its rate of change and drives all seven joints on one
shared timing $\sigma(t)\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q}(t) = \mathbf{q}^{(k)} + \sigma(t)\bigl(\mathbf{q}^\star - \mathbf{q}^{(k)}\bigr),
  \label{eq:phase-main}
\end{equation}
so the commanded pose stays on the straight line between endpoints; it
replaced a per-joint step limiter (\cref{sec:clamp-maths}). Replaying
recorded commands on the physical arm tested two models of the
simulation-to-real gap -- a fixed stop-short, $q_{\text{real}} =
q_{\text{cmd}} - \varepsilon\,\mathrm{sign}(\Delta q)$, or an error growing
with distance, $q_{\text{real}} = (1-k)\,q_{\text{cmd}}$ -- which differ only
on short moves, so joint errors were propagated to the hand,
$\Delta\mathbf{p} \approx \mathbf{J}\,\Delta\mathbf{q}$, to choose
(\cref{sec:sim-to-real}). The positioning error budget combines its terms
$b_i$ as
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\text{RSS}} = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i b_i^2}, \qquad
  \sigma_{\text{worst}} = \textstyle\sum_i b_i,
  \label{eq:budget-main}
\end{equation}
the second being the honest figure against the \SI{30}{\milli\metre} gate,
since the two largest terms are fixed offsets.

\section{Verification methodology}

Twenty-six times a result that looked like a system failure was a defect in
the measuring tool (\cref{ch:instrument}, \cref{sec:validity}). So every
analysis script carries a test with a known correct answer; synthetic data
is used only where the truth is \emph{constructed}, never rendered; and a
result that contradicts an earlier measurement is a suspect instrument
until reconciled. Because the joint solver (TRAC-IK~\cite{beeson2015tracik})
starts from a random guess, every reachability claim is ten repeats along a
path sampled every \SI{20}{\milli\metre}, and a pose that fails any repeat
is not reported reachable. Each of the perception pipeline's thirty-three
stages likewise returns a result, a refusal with a reason, or ``not
applicable'' -- never a silent zero (\cref{fig:pipeline-status}).

\section{Ethics}
\label{sec:ethics}

\textbf{This study was approved by Imperial College London's Science,
Engineering and Technology Research Ethics Committee, approval number
7111986. All participants gave informed consent to taking part and to the
use of their data.} \Cref{app:ethics} analyses the work under the
Engineering Council's four pillars and the department's six domains.
\emph{Human subjects}: the wearer bears a risk they cannot control, so
consent is taken from operator and wearer separately, in separate rooms;
the wearer holds their own emergency stop and rehearses it before any data;
a stop is data, never a lost participant; worn operation was not sought for
approval until the mount clash of \cref{sec:against-objectives} is fixed.
\emph{Scientific integrity}: every number carries its evidence class and
twenty-six instrument defects are reported. \emph{Institutional integrity}:
identities are held on encrypted College storage outside the repository,
and \repo{write\_manifest()} refuses to record a name. \emph{Social
responsibility}: assistance that helps an operator while eroding a wearer's
trust shifts harm onto the person least able to refuse it -- why H3 and H4
exist.

\section{The pilot study}

Five operators (P1--P5) each completed two to seven recorded sessions --
following a held object, reaching a target, matching a pose -- once under
direct VR control and once with assistance, from the desk arrangement of
\cref{sec:desk} (\cref{fig:pilot-procedure}). Positions, clutch state and
the robot's lag were logged
twenty times a second with an event log of every clutch press and emergency
stop, so completion time, robot path, re-grips, steering fraction and lag
came from the record, not a stopwatch. Three further operators (M1--M3)
attempted the same tasks from the mannequin master. The pilot has no
separate wearer, so H3--H5 remain for \cref{app:study}.
