\chapter{Perception, environment mapping and autonomous manipulation}
\label{ch:perception}

The system described so far commands the arm from a human input. This chapter
covers the parts added afterwards that let it command the arm from a
\emph{measurement} instead: object detection, grasp synthesis, a sweep that
measures the working surface and the objects on it, a planner that consults
the result, and a natural-language route into the same task layer. It closes
with the camera that observes the wearer, which is a safety component rather
than a manipulation one.

\section{The defect that motivated it}

Almost everything in the delivered task layer runs on \emph{declared}
coordinates. The blocks in the pick-and-place task are at their coordinates
because a source file says so; the working surface is at its height because a
constant says so. All of it is verified at ten repeats over densified paths
(\cref{ch:results}), and none of it survives a different table, a different
object, or the same object displaced by \SI{50}{\milli\metre}. The number in
the file becomes a proposition the arm acts on with complete confidence, and
nothing in the system is positioned to disagree with it.

Three components capable of sensing the same quantities already existed and
had never been composed. \repo{srl\_perception/table\_scene.py} finds a
support plane and the objects resting on it, and was used to print a
description. \repo{srl\_perception/joint\_planner.py} defines a
\texttt{VoxelWorld} so that a planner can avoid obstacles that are not the
wearer, and was constructed nowhere outside its own self-test, which means no
path this project had ever planned knew that the table existed.
\repo{srl\_experiments/work\_surface.py} exposes \texttt{set\_measured()} so
that the surface height can come from depth, and nothing called it.

\section{Detection}

Detection is open-vocabulary rather than class-fixed: the stage takes a noun
phrase and returns instances. \repo{srl\_perception/prompt\_detector.py} runs
YOLO-World for boxes~\cite{cheng2024yoloworld} with FastSAM for
masks~\cite{zhao2023fastsam}, and falls back to a hue-and-depth filter when
neither model is resident. \textbf{The backend that produced a detection is
reported with the detection}, so a result can never imply tacitly that the
stronger model was running.

Two properties of the deployment are worth recording because both were forced
by measurement rather than chosen.

\emph{Range is used as a discriminator where hue cannot be.} The laboratory
contains teal robots whose shadowed hue is \num{76}; the target green block
sits at \num{74}. Two units apart is inside the noise, and a colour filter had
already been fooled by them three times. The robots stand \SI{2.8}{\metre}
away while the rig works within \SI{1.6}{\metre}, so range separates the two
populations cleanly and is applied at detection time.

\emph{Grasp synthesis is geometric and deliberately not learned.}
\repo{srl\_perception/rgbd\_grasp.py} takes the principal axes of the depth
cloud, closes across the short axis and approaches along the free one. A
parallel jaw closing on a rigid convex object is a solved problem; a learned
alternative would need video memory that is not available beside the detector;
and, decisively, a geometric method can \emph{name} the cause of a refusal. It
does: no depth returns, wider than the \SI{85}{\milli\metre} jaw, or too few
points to fit an axis.

\subsection{A reachability gate that could not fail}

\repo{srl\_perception/grasp\_pipeline.py} checks a candidate grasp for reach
and for wearer clearance before offering it. The clearance half of that check
was inoperative for the life of the module, and the mechanism is the same one
that recurs throughout this thesis.

The scorer returns two clearances. One is taken over the whole kinematic
chain, including the immobile mount stub between \texttt{base\_link} and
\texttt{shoulder\_link}; that stub is nearer the wearer than anything the
joints can move, so the quantity reads a constant \SI{0.2202}{\metre} in every
pose the arm can adopt. The gate compared it against the \SI{0.15}{\metre}
floor. \textbf{Every grasp this pipeline ever approved was tested against a
predicate that could not be false.} It now reads the moving-chain clearance,
which is \SI{0.4031}{\metre} at the home pose, and a regression test aims the
hand at the centre of the wearer's torso and requires a refusal that names the
wearer.

\section{Measuring the working area}

\subsection{Why the sweep holds one wrist attitude}

The first version of the sweep aimed the tool axis at each grid point in turn.
That is the intuitive design and it is wrong for a measurement. Re-aiming
means the wrist attitude differs between neighbouring cells
\SI{90}{\milli\metre} apart, so a residual error in the camera-to-wrist
transform becomes a \emph{different} error in every frame: it can neither
cancel across views nor be estimated from them.

A printer's bed probe does not re-aim. It holds one attitude and translates.
\repo{srl\_perception/calibration\_sweep.py} does the same. One wrist
direction per pass, with the camera grid being the work grid rigidly
translated back along that fixed ray, so consecutive cells differ by a pure
translation; several passes at different yaws supply the object sides, which a
single elevation cannot see. The sweep is two layers by three yaws by
twenty-four cells by two arms, and the traverse order is a boustrophedon
rather than a typewriter raster: \SI{1.140}{\metre} of travel against
\SI{1.702}{\metre} for the same coverage, measured by the module's own
self-test rather than asserted.

\subsection{Stationary is not arrived}

A joint can sit \SI{0.02}{\radian} from its commanded value and still be
moving through it. A frame captured at that moment is smeared across two
poses, while every statistic computed downstream reports normally. The sweep
therefore requires no joint to move by more than \SI{0.0015}{\radian} over
\SI{400}{\milli\second}, which is a tenth of the arrival tolerance, before a
frame is accepted.

Until an incremental re-map exists, \repo{scripts/pick\_from\_map.py} reports
the map's age on every run and \textbf{refuses to move from a map older than

driving the arm at coordinates measured in a previous session is the failure
that costs something, and a stale file is indistinguishable from a fresh one
by inspection.

\section{Planning against the measurement}

For teleoperation there is no planner and there should not be: the operator's
hand is the plan and the correct problem is smooth, safe following
(\cref{ch:method}). For the autonomous mode there is a goal, and the absence
of a planner had a measurable cost. A pick whose pre-grasp and grasp poses are
both individually clear, streamed as a straight line in joint space, passes
within \SI{0.1462}{\metre} of the wearer at step twenty-nine of sixty-four.
The path checker catches it, and catching it means the run stops.

\repo{joint\_planner.py} implements RRT-Connect~\cite{kuffner2000rrtconnect}
with four choices made for this platform specifically.

\begin{description}
  \item[Joint space, not Cartesian.] The binding constraint is the distance
    from the whole \emph{arm} to the wearer, not from the hand. A Cartesian
    planner would solve inverse kinematics at every node in any case.
  \item[The wearer floor is the collision predicate], evaluated through the
    same moving-chain clearance the guard enforces, rather than through the
    semantic description, which is blind to the proximal pairs that matter.
  \item[Every edge densified at \SI{0.05}{\radian}], matching the path
    checker. A planner that checks only its nodes reproduces, one level up,
    the defect it exists to remove.
  \item[Straight line first, then shortcut, then resample] to the follower's
    own step size. Handing the follower waypoints spaced further apart than
    its maximum step means it invents the intermediate motion by unchecked
    interpolation, which reintroduces the same defect at the last stage.
\end{description}

Larger alternatives were assessed against the platform's video memory and
rejected on stated grounds rather than by omission. CUDA-accelerated motion
generation is the strongest option in this space and needs roughly
\SI{2.5}{\gibi\byte} at batch 1024 on a card with \SI{48}{\gibi\byte};
GPU-accelerated simulation for policy learning lists an eight-gigabyte
card as its minimum~\cite{isaaclab}. This machine has \SI{4}{\gibi\byte} in
total, shared with the detector.

\section{Commanding a task in natural language}

The full-autonomy mode accepts a typed or spoken instruction. The parser is a
grammar, for the reasons given in \cref{ch:method}, and the design question
that matters is what it does when an instruction is ambiguous.

Asked to \emph{put the blue block on the blue mat} with two blue blocks
present, the system \textbf{asks} rather than choosing. Nearest, leftmost and
first-detected are all defensible and all guesses, and the manipulator is
bolted to a person. Asked to \emph{put the blue ones on the blue mat}, it does
not ask, because the plural settles it. The distinction lives in the
instruction's own quantity marker rather than in a heuristic over how many
candidates happen to match, which is why the evaluation scene contains two
blocks of each colour rather than one.

The bottom row is the one that matters, and the three misunderstandings share
a mechanism worth stating. Each was a compound instruction of the form
\emph{put the blue ones on the blue mat and the green ones on the green mat}:
two complete instructions sharing a verb. The destination split cut at the
first destination cue, so the head of the sentence was \emph{put the blue
ones}; the two-target check counted targets within the head and found one; and
the planner emitted a plan for the first clause, discarded the second in
silence, moved two objects of four, and reported success. Every component
behaved as specified.

A refusal costs the operator a repetition. A misunderstanding moves the arm,
and it is therefore the quantity this evaluation is designed around.

\section{Observing the wearer}
\label{sec:wearer-tracking}

The clearance floor of \cref{ch:method} is enforced against a body. Until late
in the project that body was always the mannequin in the robot description.
\repo{srl\_perception/wearer\_tracker\_node.py} replaces it, part by part,
with a measured one, using a markerless pose model with metric landmarks
lifted by perspective-n-point~\cite{lugaresi2019mediapipe}.

The safety argument for admitting a camera into the clearance check is a
single monotonicity rule, and it is enforced in one function:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The camera may only ever make the wearer larger.} Per body part,
\texttt{fuse()} retains whichever of the tracked and mannequin primitives is
\emph{nearer} the robot. There is no mode switch.
\end{quote}

A body injected \SI{0.10}{\metre}, \SI{0.30}{\metre}, \SI{1.00}{\metre} and
\SI{5.00}{\metre} further away in turn moves the enforced margin by nothing at
all. Six faults were then injected through the live node, comprising a dark
frame, a blank frame, two people in shot, an empty frame, occluded arms, and a
control; the node refused seven of seven cases, and each refusal was required
to \emph{name} the injected fault. That naming requirement is what exposed a
false pass in which the two-person gate had never executed.

The substitution is per part, so a measured torso can stand beside a mannequin
upper arm within one scene, and \texttt{/wearer/enforced} publishes which body
is in force. Before this was connected, the tracker reached the planning scene
and \emph{not} \repo{mount\_guard\_node}, which constructs its wearer at
import time: the body the planner avoided and the body the floor was enforced
against were two different bodies, and the floor held the mannequin.

The honest limitation is on the fallback side and is quantified in
\cref{ch:discussion}. If the wearer raises their arms and tracking is then
lost, the guard reverts to the assumed posture, which has the arms down, and
believes there is \SI{0.317}{\metre} of clearance where there is
\SI{0.093}{\metre}.

\section{The scene camera}

A second camera, observing the workspace from outside the arms rather than
from a wrist, is implemented. \repo{scene\_camera\_node} refuses to republish
rather than passing through stale frames and publishes a zero intrinsic matrix
until calibrated; both calibration tools carry self-tests that pass to
\SI{0.43}{\percent} in focal length, \SI{4.5}{px} in principal point and
\SI{0.0000}{\metre} and \SI{0.000}{\degree} in the extrinsic.

\textbf{No camera has ever been attached to it.} The device is the host's own
webcam, and forwarding it into the Linux environment requires an
administrator-level bind that was refused. Everything in this section is
verified against mock inputs, and the project keeps ``as built'' and
``inferred'' in separate records for this reason.
