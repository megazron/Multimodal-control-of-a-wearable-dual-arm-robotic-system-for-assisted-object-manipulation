\chapter{System as built}
\label{ch:method}

This chapter describes the delivered system: how it is decomposed into
packages, how a master pose becomes an arm command, what happens when sensing
degrades, and what can stop the arm. \Cref{ch:master} covered the master
hardware and firmware; this chapter starts where the serial frame arrives.

\section{Architecture}

The workspace is six packages with a one-way dependency
graph (\cref{fig:arch}). The direction is a design constraint rather than a
convention. Direct teleoperation is the baseline condition of every planned
experiment, so if the teleoperation package imported anything from the
autonomy package the comparison would be circular. This was verified two ways:
no such import exists anywhere in the source, and the inverse-kinematics
follower still reaches its running state with the autonomy and perception
packages physically deleted from the install tree.

\section{From master pose to arm command}

\subsection{Spherical decomposition}

The commanded position is built in spherical form about the master's shoulder
rather than by forward kinematics from seven joint angles.
\Cref{ch:development} gives the measurements that forced the change. Each
component comes from whichever sensor observes it most directly:

\begin{align}
  \phi &= \arcsin\!\left(\hat{a} \cdot \frac{\mathbf{g}}{\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert}\right)
    &&\text{elevation, from the gravity vector} \label{eq:elev}\\
  \psi &= \theta_1 - \theta_1^{0}
    &&\text{azimuth, from the shoulder roll} \label{eq:azim}\\
  r &= \bigl\lVert \mathrm{FK}(\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3, \theta_4, 0, 0, 0) \bigr\rVert
    &&\text{radius, magnitude only} \label{eq:reach}
\end{align}

Here $\hat{a}$ is the arm's long axis expressed in the sensor's own frame,
captured once with the master hanging at rest, and $\mathbf{g}$ is the
measured acceleration.

Three properties follow, and each addresses a specific failure of the previous
formulation. \Cref{eq:elev} is mount-independent, because $\hat{a}$ is
captured through the same mounting it is later used with, and drift-free,
because it integrates nothing. \Cref{eq:azim} depends on one directly measured
zero-referenced quantity rather than on four compounded ones.
\Cref{eq:reach} takes a magnitude, which is dominated by the bend joints and
insensitive to errors in the rolls, so a dead roll channel can be passed
through as zero without corrupting it.

The sign convention in \cref{eq:elev} is asserted at capture time: hanging is
\SI{-90}{\degree}, horizontal is \SI{0}{\degree}, vertical is
\SI{+90}{\degree}. The specification originally called for a negation in both
$\hat{a}$ and the elevation expression, which compound and place the resting
pose at \SI{+90}{\degree}, inverting the vertical axis alone. Only one
negation is correct.

\subsection{Anchoring, scaling and indexing}

The commanded end-effector position is

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{anchor}} + s\,\bigl(\mathbf{d} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{ref}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}

with $\mathbf{d}$ the master tip in the robot frame, $\mathbf{d}_{\text{ref}}$
the reference latched at clutch engagement, and $s$ a per-arm scale.

The reference is latched from the first \emph{valid} frame after boot, at the
master's actual pose. An earlier version initialised it to the origin of the
master's frame, which made the first command $\mathbf{p}_{\text{anchor}} +
s\,\mathbf{d}$: a step of the master's entire tip vector, roughly
\SI{0.22}{\metre}, in whatever direction the master happened to be lying. The
only master pose that did not produce a jump was $\mathbf{d} = 0$, which is
why the procedure previously required the operator to hold a calibrated
neutral before start-up. Requiring validity is not incidental. Latching from a
frame that failed validation offsets every subsequent command.

Scale is re-read every cycle so it can be retuned live, and a change re-bases
the anchor so the commanded pose stays continuous:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{\text{anchor}} \mathrel{+}= (s_{\text{old}} - s_{\text{new}})\,
  \bigl(\mathbf{d} - \mathbf{d}_{\text{ref}}\bigr).
  \label{eq:rebase}
\end{equation}

Without \cref{eq:rebase} a scale change displaces the arm by
$(s_{\text{new}} - s_{\text{old}})$ times the current displacement, which is
largest exactly when the operator is furthest from the engagement point.

Releasing the clutch freezes the command; re-engaging re-latches
$\mathbf{d}_{\text{ref}}$ at the master's new pose. Successive cycles
therefore accumulate, and the reachable set is bounded by the robot rather
than by the master's range. Engagement is gated on the master being
quasi-static, because latching a reference while the master is moving captures
a reference that is already stale.

\section{Graceful degradation}

\subsection{Channel health}

Each channel carries a verdict computed from its own recent history, over
\emph{distinct} sensor updates rather than over transmitted rows. The
distinction is not pedantic: at the frame rate roughly
\SIrange{82}{88}{\percent} of consecutive rows repeat, so any statistic
computed across rows is dominated by duplicates. Every channel statistic
computed that way returned a median absolute difference of exactly zero, which
was read for a long time as evidence of dead channels.

Four verdicts are assigned, in order:

\begin{description}
  \item[Dead.] Circular range below \SI{5}{\degree}, or dropouts above
    \SI{90}{\percent}.
  \item[Incoherent.] More than \SI{5}{\percent} of updates jump by more than
    \SI{60}{\degree}, which at the observed update rate is roughly
    \SI{2500}{\degree\per\second}.
  \item[Intermittent.] Dropouts above \SI{2}{\percent}.
  \item[Alive.] Coherent, range at least \SI{20}{\degree}, dropouts at most
    \SI{2}{\percent}.
\end{description}

The incoherent verdict is the one that did not exist in earlier versions and
is the one that matters. A failing potentiometer does not go quiet. It returns
values spanning a wide interval which are not a trajectory, with consecutive
updates unrelated to one another. Range alone calls that healthy, which is how
a broken channel keeps being trusted.

Range must be computed as the smallest arc containing every sample. Unwrapping
and subtracting is invalid for a rotary sensor, because across a dropout the
unwrap cannot know how far the joint moved and the accumulated offset runs
away; that method reported \SI{314363}{\degree} on a \SI{360}{\degree} sensor.
Samples outside $[0, 360]$ are rejected first, since a small fraction are
serial parse glitches arriving in bursts.

\subsection{Mode-scoped validation}

Validation rejects a frame only on the channels the active position mode
consumes. In spherical mode those are joints one, two and four. A dead joint
three, five, six or seven cannot reject a frame, which is the entire purpose:
the arm keeps working with four of its seven channels gone.

A rejected frame holds the last good value rather than substituting a
fabricated one. The cost is staleness rather than error, and it is charged
honestly: a burst of dropouts freezes the command for the duration of the
burst, which flattens measured displacement and adds apparent lag.

\Cref{fig:degradation} gives the measured cost of each reduction, against

are mechanical rather than statistical.

The last rung is the design decision. With no well-conditioned channels the
node reports position unavailable and publishes that state, rather than
extrapolating a position from what remains. A fabricated position on a
manipulator bolted to a person is the worst available outcome, and it is
indistinguishable downstream from a real one unless the distinction is made
explicit at the source.

\subsection{Reduced-degree-of-freedom fallback}

When both bend joints of an arm are unavailable, the forward-kinematic
magnitude in \cref{eq:reach} becomes constant and the arm's commanded set
collapses from a shell of finite thickness to a surface. Elevation and azimuth
are unaffected, because they are angular and come from elsewhere.

The fallback restores the missing dimension on a channel that is alive,
coherent, and not otherwise consumed. Deflection of the wrist roll past a
deadband commands a \emph{rate} of change of radius rather than an absolute
radius. Rate rather than position is necessary because that joint's usable
range is small, so an absolute mapping would give coarse resolution over the
whole radial extent and would require the operator to hold a wrist angle to
hold a radius.

The channel choice is constrained. The gripper is on the force sensors and the
clutch is on the buttons, so both are unavailable without conflict. The wrist
roll conflicts with nothing, which means the operator can extend while
gripping and while indexing.

the dependency graph. \Cref{tab:modes} summarises them; the four that are

Orientation assist is a stub and is marked as one. It would supply wrist
orientation under an orientation constraint, and the inverse-kinematics plugin
in use ignores the constraint field entirely: measured over 270 solver calls,
success was identical to the digit with and without the constraint. It needs a
constraint-aware plugin or explicit yaw sampling.

One safety stack serves all six. Every mode transition asserts the invariant,
and dropping any one of the six shared mechanisms is refused in every mode
rather than being available as a per-mode option.

\subsection{Why the transport for VR is USB rather than the network}

The headset connects over \texttt{adb reverse} on USB, so the client addresses
\texttt{ws://127.0.0.1}. Browser-based immersive sessions require a secure
context, which over a local network means a self-signed certificate carrying
the address, a firewall exception, and an operator accepting a warning inside
a headset they are wearing. A loopback address is a secure origin by
definition, so all of that disappears. The link is also not the wireless
network the arms are on.

\subsection{Why the voice mode uses a grammar and not a language model}

The command set is closed and small: grab, handover, place, stop. A quantised
seven-billion-parameter model would occupy three to four of the four gigabytes
of video memory available on this machine and add several hundred milliseconds,
and it would place a non-deterministic component in the path that decides
whether a robot beside a person's head begins moving.

The failure modes differ in kind, which is the substantive argument. A grammar
that does not recognise an utterance returns unparsed, and the robot asks
again. A language model asked for structured output can return a different,
plausible verb, and nothing downstream can distinguish that from a correct
parse. For five verbs that is a poor exchange.

\subsection{Why not an end-to-end vision-language-action policy}

Three reasons, recorded so the decision is not silently reversed. Such models
are trained on embodiment collections that contain no backpack-mounted dual
Gen3, and zero-shot transfer to a novel embodiment is poor while fine-tuning
needs demonstration data this project does not have. They emit actions
directly, which bypasses every gate in this system, since collision-aware
inverse kinematics, the clearance floor and the step guard all sit between a
\emph{pose} and the arm. And their failures are unattributable, where the
modular pipeline fails at a named stage with its inputs logged.

\section{Safety architecture}

\subsection{Collision avoidance in three stages}

The wearer is in the collision model. Adding this was not a matter of setting
a flag: the flag was already set, but the wearer's model carried
twenty-seven visual elements and one collision element, and no wearer link
appeared in the semantic description. A planner cannot avoid what it cannot
see.

Thirteen collision elements were added, paired with thirty-six exclusions for
the mount-adjacent proximal links. The arm bases are bolted to the harness and
are permanently adjacent, so without those exclusions every pose is trivially
in collision. Everything distal of the first arm link stays enabled, which is
what actually protects the wearer.

Avoidance is graduated. Collision-aware inverse kinematics rejects a
configuration; on rejection the redundant degree of freedom is re-seeded and
the solve retried, which recovers poses that would otherwise be dropped; and a
geometric clearance floor measured from the transform tree provides a final
margin. Driving the commanded pose directly at the wearer's head and torso and
holding it there stopped the arm at \SIrange{0.072}{0.079}{\metre} with zero
clearance-floor blocks, so the earlier stages did the work and the floor
remained a backstop. Every zone resumed on retreat.

\subsection{Blocking must be observable}

Four mechanisms in this system have historically stopped all motion silently.
The common failure was never the blocking, which is usually correct. It was
that blocking looked identical to working.

Every mechanism that can stop motion now registers with a monitor that
enforces three properties: it announces itself by name on the first block and
repeats; sustained blocking escalates in severity, because a block held for
seconds is never normal; and a recovery string is mandatory at registration,
so a blocker that cannot say how it clears is a latch and registration is
refused.

\subsection{Expiry is not permission}

Every blocker in the system is written as a guard that blocks and returns, and
clears on the fall-through path. That is correct while the guard is mutated
elsewhere, and it latches the moment the guard also stops the callback
running. This happened five times, the fifth inside the code written to catch
the first four.

The structural fix is auto-expiry: a blocker not re-asserted for two seconds
is treated as abandoned rather than held, since a genuinely held condition is
re-asserted every cycle by design. Expiry is a third state, not a clear one.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Blocker states. Expiry means the asserting loop stopped running, so
  the guarded condition is unknown rather than absent.}
  \label{tab:blockstates}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
    \toprule
    State & Meaning & Treated as blocking \\
    \midrule
    active  & the condition is held & yes \\
    expired & the asserting loop stopped; condition \textbf{unknown} & \textbf{yes} \\
    clear   & live code says the condition is gone & no \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Reading an expiry as clear would turn a crashed guard into a green light,
which is worse than the latch it replaces. Two placement rules follow and both
are load-bearing. The follower's refusal to publish sits at the publish and
not at callback entry, because the clears that resolve an expiry are upstream
of it and refusing at entry would prevent the re-assertion that clears the
unknown. And the unknown-state blocker is excluded from its own trigger, or it
re-arms itself indefinitely.

\subsection{Emergency stop}

Three independent triggers latch until an explicit reset: a service or topic
call, both master buttons held simultaneously, and a dead-man on the master
data going stale.

The dead-man keys on the \emph{source} timestamp of the data, not on message
arrival. This is the correction that made it work at all. The master node does
not go silent when the master degrades: on a validation failure it substitutes
the last good value and keeps publishing at full rate. It used to stamp that
substituted frame with the publication time, so on the failure mode that
actually occurs, a board emitting corrupted values rather than vanishing, the
topic never went quiet and the timestamp never went stale. A dead-man keyed on
arrival could not fire. Only a clean unplug produced silence, which is why
every cable-pull test passed.

The first task of the set is a positioning task with three target widths at
each amplitude, which characterises the interface against the
speed-accuracy relation~\cite{fitts1954information} and provides the
single-arm against both-arms contrast that measures the attention bottleneck
directly. \Cref{fig:expdesign} gives the design and \cref{fig:timeline} the
session structure.

\section{Verification methodology}

Sixteen times in this project a result that looked like a system failure was
a defect in the measuring tool. \Cref{ch:instrument} reports all sixteen, the
four mechanisms behind them, and the finding that nine of sixteen were caught
by an independently known quantity while only three were caught by inspection.
The rule below is what came out of them. A noise floor of exactly zero on all fourteen
channels was the recorder sampling faster than the sensor updates. An
isotropy figure swinging by a factor of two between runs was a statistic
chosen to be maximally sensitive to a rare tail failure. A detection rate of
\SIrange{0}{4}{\percent} was a renderer producing images unlike anything the
detector was trained on. A path-verification failure was the verifier carrying
its test path in the one direction that leaves the feasible region.

The rule adopted is that a surprising failure is evidence about the instrument
until the instrument has been cleared against a known answer. In practice this
means every analysis script has a known-answer test, real recorded data is
preferred to generated data, synthetic data is used only where the ground
truth is constructed rather than rendered, and a result contradicting an
earlier measurement is treated as an instrument check until the two are
reconciled.

A corollary governs every coordinate in this thesis. The inverse-kinematics
solver restarts randomly, so a pose at the edge of the feasible set is a coin
flip and one call is one flip. Verification is therefore ten repeats over the
whole path, densified to \SI{20}{\milli\metre}, not two calls at the
endpoints. Poses are graded solid, marginal or failed, and marginal is not
usable.
